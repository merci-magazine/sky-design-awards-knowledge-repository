## Editorial Rewrite

The Private Club as a Sales Narrative
Property sales galleries are usually designed for immediate clarity: bright light, polished surfaces and an abundance of information. OFT gives The MVP a more seductive beginning. Conceived as a sequence of cinematic rooms, the 644-square-metre sales office exchanges commercial brightness for shadow, deep timber and the measured glamour of a private club. Its mood feels established rather than newly manufactured, as though the interior has already acquired its own rituals and memories.
Arrival is choreographed as a change of pace. Visitors descend from the glare of Hong Kong through a staircase lined with slender timber fins and narrow bands of light. The compressed ceiling and intermittent illumination allow the eye to adjust gradually, turning an ordinary transition between floors into the opening scene of the experience. At its base, the plan expands and the atmosphere becomes more theatrical: burgundy textile walls, dark joinery, warm metal and precisely illuminated steps introduce the development through anticipation rather than display alone.
The interior’s sense of permanence is built through detail. Recessed timber coffers give the ceiling depth and rhythm. Curved upholstered panels meet timber bases through fine metallic trims; textured glass glows from concealed light sources; polished fittings catch small flashes of reflection against the darker surfaces. A suspended composition of glass forms creates a brighter moment above the entrance, recalling the sparkle of a historic cinema foyer without reproducing one literally. Character accumulates through repeated junctions, controlled colour and the way each material receives light.
Deeper inside, the spatial language softens. Rounded walls, fluted upholstery and low, generous seating create rooms with the privacy of a members’ lounge. Burgundy, tobacco brown, cream and muted gold carry a cultivated, faintly nostalgic elegance, while velvet and carpet temper sound for quieter conversations. These are practical decisions for a sales environment, yet they also give the project its emotional register: clients are invited to linger, imagine and discuss a future home without feeling hurried through a transaction.
The circular presentation room brings the concept into focus. A luminous, textured-glass counter sits beneath concentric ceiling lines, surrounded by individually framed records, screens and turntables. The room reads partly as an archive, partly as a listening salon and partly as stage. It gives The MVP a cultural personality beyond conventional property branding, connecting architecture with music, collecting and the pleasure of private leisure.
OFT’s achievement lies in making a sales office feel less temporary than the function usually implies. Its cinematic character comes from sequence and timing; its quiet luxury comes from material weight, acoustic comfort and exacting workmanship. The project sells no single decorative style. It presents a way of living, private, cultivated and rich in atmosphere, hen allows visitors to imagine themselves entering the story.

## Editor’s Review

The Timber Afterlight Lounge is distinguished by the consistency with which OFT converts a commercial sales journey into an architectural narrative. Circulation, lighting, acoustic treatment and material detailing work together to slow the visitor’s pace and establish privacy before any formal presentation begins. The coffered timber ceilings, curved upholstered walls, illuminated glass and finely controlled metal trims create a cultivated sense of permanence that is unusual for a temporary sales environment. The listening-salon presentation room extends the project beyond conventional property branding, giving The MVP a cultural identity through music, collecting and hospitality. Its success lies in making the atmosphere operational: every dramatic effect also supports orientation, conversation or attention.

## Peggy’s Editor’s Note

Luxury is often communicated through brightness, scale and recognisable objects. OFT takes a more assured position. The Timber Afterlight Lounge feels valuable because it appears to have a past. Deep timber, burgundy textile, muted metal and pools of amber light recall the privacy of an established club and the anticipation of entering an old cinema, without turning the interior into a period set. The details carry this impression: metallic bands complete the curved fabric panels, glass fittings throw small points of light across dark surfaces, and the coffered ceilings give each room a measured rhythm. The listening salon is the decisive gesture. Records, screens and turntables transform a property presentation into an encounter with taste, leisure and collecting. Clients are not simply shown a development; they are invited to recognise a possible version of themselves within it. This is where the project moves beyond sales design and becomes relevant to a wider lifestyle audience.

## Original Designer Submission — Verbatim

The Timber Afterlight Lounge
The story of The MVP Sales Office began with a reaction to the typical sales gallery—bright, flat, and forgettable. Here, the design team imagined the opposite: a place where clients feel as if they are stepping into a film, moving through a sequence of scenes that quietly reveal the character of The MVP.
The experience starts at the threshold. Visitors leave the glare of the city and enter a world of lowered ceilings, softened light, and deep timber tones. A staircase draws them downward, lined with vertical ribbons of illumination that appear and disappear between slim wooden fins. Each step is a beat in the rhythm of the journey, a moment for the eye to adjust and the mind to shift into a more contemplative pace.
At the base of the stairs, the space opens up like a stage. Overhead, a gridded ceiling and precise downlights cast subtle highlights on stone and metal, while walls of warm timber wrap around the room. The atmosphere is deliberately quiet—not silent, but composed—so conversations can unfold naturally. A circular counter, edged with textured glass and grounded by solid stone, becomes the focal point where plans are introduced, models are studied, and first impressions turn into real possibilities.
As clients move deeper inside, the narrative softens. A lounge corner, wrapped in dark tactile walls and furnished with plush, low-slung seating, feels almost like a private screening room. Light is kept low and warm, glowing from concealed fixtures, table lamps, and the shimmer of metallic accents. In these cocooned spaces, the noise of decision-making fades, leaving room for reflection and more intimate dialogue.
Across the 644-square-meter interior, every surface is chosen for the way it receives and reflects light: brushed metals that catch a passing glow, velvety fabrics that absorb sound, richly grained timbers that ground the composition. Rather than chasing overt spectacle, the design uses shadow and contrast to suggest a quieter, more enduring idea of luxury. The result is a sales office that does more than present a property—it choreographs an experience, leaving visitors with the feeling that they have just walked through the opening scenes of their own future story at The MVP.
Developed for The MVP, Emperor Properties
/ spatial planning, interior design & site supervision
/ 644 square metres
