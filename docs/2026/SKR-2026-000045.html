Gifu-Jo Rakuichi

SKR-2026-000045 is the Sky Knowledge Record for Gifu-Jo Rakuichi, designed by SEMBA Corporation, located in Gifu Park, Gifu, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Gifu-Jo Rakuichi designed by SEMBA Corporation transforms Gifu Park into a bustling tourism and hospitality hub, increasing visitor dwell time through a strategic network of timber pavilions and communal gathering spaces.

Editorial

Gifu Castle has historically served as one of the most recognizable landmarks within the city, positioned atop Mount Kinka and intrinsically linked to Oda Nobunaga. The castle draws nearly one million visitors annually. Nevertheless, the experience at ground level has been markedly brief, with approximately 80 percent of visitors departing the area within one hour due to a lack of dining, relaxation, and engaging spaces prior to continuing their journey. In response to this disparity between cultural significance and everyday experience, SEMBA Corporation has developed Gifu-Jo Rakuichi.

This initiative is grounded in the philosophy of “Savouring Gifu and enjoying OMOTENASHI hospitality.” Its objectives are as civic as they are touristic: to revitalize Gifu Park into a venue where visitors, residents, historical context, and contemporary commerce can interact seamlessly at the entrance to Gifu Castle.

SEMBA's design approach draws inspiration from the site’s historical role as a transportation hub. The project features a dispersed array of single-storey timber structures arranged along existing park pathways, evoking the vibrant character of a Sengoku-period castle town. This arrangement fosters an open environment that allows mature trees, Mount Kinka, the three-storey pagoda, and vistas of Gifu Castle to enhance the visitor experience.

This urban composition emphasizes the importance of time spent in the area, rather than merely facilitating transit. The layout includes shops, food vendors, seating areas, and shaded spots to encourage visitors to pause between destinations. This intention is equally applicable to local residents, who may utilize the park as an integral part of their daily lives rather than as a mere tourist attraction. SEMBA characterizes this space as a “town living room,” where the activities of tourism and local community life can converge.

The architectural design supports this informality. With deep eaves, timber frames, and open shopfronts, each pavilion maintains a distinct identity while contributing to a cohesive visual ensemble. Lawns and trees intersperse the buildings, and the circulation pattern remains sufficiently open to allow individuals to navigate freely among retail areas, food options, historical landmarks, and open spaces without being confined to a singular commercial route. Moreover, the scale of the project imparts a broader lesson. Gifu-Jo Rakuichi does not rely on an imposing visitor center to communicate the city’s heritage; rather, historical narratives are woven into the very fabric of the environment: the dispersed layout, pedestrian-friendly streets, connection to the mountain, and the atmosphere of a small settlement beneath the castle.

SEMBA's engagement in this project encompassed consulting, master planning, design criteria, supervision, interiors, and signage, ensuring that the commercial program and public realm developed as an integrated system.

Gifu-Jo Rakuichi introduces a new social dynamic to Gifu Park. Visitors may still arrive for the castle, yet the journey now accommodates opportunities for dining, conversation, shopping, rest, and repeated visits.
