SKR-2026-000041

Grid Coffee in Sanyuanli Market

SKR-2026-000041 is the Sky Knowledge Record for Grid Coffee in Sanyuanli Market, designed by B.L.U.E. Architecture Studio, located in Sanyuanli Food Market, Beijing, China. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

B.L.U.E. Architecture Studio fits a 25-square-metre Grid Coffee into Beijing’s Sanyuanli Food Market, pairing rough aggregate concrete with hand-worked copper and a sculptural counter shaped for stand-and-sip service.

Editorial

Coffee at the Market Threshold

At the northern entrance to Beijing’s Sanyuanli Food Market, Grid Coffee occupies only 25 square metres yet holds two public faces. One addresses the street through a deep concrete portal; the other opens directly into the market through a compact selling window. B.L.U.E. Architecture Studio treats both edges as active pieces of the neighbourhood.

The street entrance is framed in rough, ribbed concrete and closed with hand-assembled copper doors. Their warm, irregular surface catches the light against the weight of the mineral surround. Inside the market, a hammered copper shutter operates at the scale of a stall: open, it becomes a counter for exchange; closed, it reads as a quiet metal panel set within the textured wall.

A near-right-angled bar organises the narrow interior. Ordering, preparation and standing consumption follow its two arms, while recessed ledges give customers a place to pause without introducing conventional seating. The pale aggregate counter contains coffee beans from the brand’s own supply, turning the working surface into a material record of the product.

The palette is deliberately compact. Sand-faced cement and exposed aggregate establish a muted field; oxidised and hammered copper bring depth; elm and other woods soften the points touched by hand and body. Small timber signs identify coffee varieties in a manner borrowed from the market’s changeable price labels.

This is a coffee shop shaped by the tempo of buying, tasting and moving on. Its windows extend the transactional life of the neighbouring stalls, while its crafted surfaces provide a moment of concentration amid the market’s colour and noise. The project’s modest scale gives each junction unusual weight: door, shutter, bar, ledge and display all participate in the exchange between coffee culture and everyday commerce.
