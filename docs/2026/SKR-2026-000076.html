GORAKADAN FUJI GOLF

SKR-2026-000076 is the Sky Knowledge Record for GORAKADAN FUJI GOLF, designed by A.N.D., NOMURA Co., Ltd., located in Mount Fuji region, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

A.N.D. NOMURA Co. is transforming GORAKADAN FUJI GOLF into a year-round wellness resort that integrates professional golf facilities, hot springs, suites, and direct views of Mount Fuji.

Editorial

Golf clubs have long been places where rituals take shape: arriving early, changing attire, spending hours outdoors, returning to the clubhouse, and sharing conversations that linger in the air. At GORA KADAN FUJI GOLF, these time-honored practices are reimagined through a uniquely Japanese lens of retreat, with the majestic presence of Mount Fuji enhancing the experience from dawn to dusk.

This project revitalizes an established golf club situated next to the soon-to-be-completed GORA KADAN FUJI hotel, transforming the traditional clubhouse into a resort-like environment. While golf remains a focal point, the experience is elevated by the integration of natural hot springs, all-day dining, cozy fireside lounges, and luxurious suite accommodations. This thoughtful combination reflects a holistic view of leisure, where sports, hospitality, and relaxation seamlessly coexist.

The most striking aspect of GORA KADAN FUJI GOLF is, without doubt, Mount Fuji itself, which is prominently visible from the site. The architecture is meticulously designed to embrace this iconic backdrop, influencing the orientation and proportions of the shared spaces. Guests are afforded numerous opportunities to engage with this landscape, which holds a significance that transcends mere tourism.

This deep connection to the surrounding natural beauty redefines the clubhouse experience. Even though golf is the initial draw, the day's activities extend far beyond the final hole. Guests may find themselves soaking in rejuvenating hot springs, enjoying a meal with breathtaking views of the mountain, or gathering around a warm fire, all contributing to a slower pace more in line with Japanese resort culture. The architectural renewal pays tribute to the legacy of the original club, retaining elements of the existing structure and brickwork while introducing culturally resonant materials like Japanese stone and timber.

Throughout the interior, historical golf clubs and archival photographs are prominently displayed, ensuring that the building's past is woven into its new context. This layering of history adds a unique depth to the project. Golf was introduced to Japan as an imported sport, bringing with it Western clubhouse conventions. At GORA KADAN FUJI GOLF, these traditions exist alongside Japanese principles of spatial intervals, material restraint, and a deep appreciation of the natural world. This balance preserves both influences without allowing either to be reduced to mere decoration.

Integral to this design ethos is the Japanese concept of "ma," which emphasizes the importance of space between elements. Rooms are thoughtfully crafted to provide visual and physical room for the changing light, weather, and seasonal colors, inviting guests to fully appreciate the dynamic character of Mount Fuji. The mountain’s appearance shifts throughout the day, sometimes standing out in sharp relief, while at other times it melds into the surrounding atmosphere.

For the designers, the relationship between interior scale and landscape stands as a key achievement of the project. Symmetry offers balance, while carefully placed openings and intervals maintain a sense of fluidity. The use of timber, stone, and preserved brick creates interiors that have substance, enabling the distant mountain to remain the focal point.

The clubhouse program is aligned with the rhythms of a full day. Arrivals, dining options, lounge spaces, suites, and terraces on the upper level all boast views of Mount Fuji, while the ground floor connects lockers and bathing facilities directly to the golf course. A new on-course teahouse provides an additional chance for reflection amid the game.

Importantly, the clubhouse’s accessibility has evolved as well. Certain dining and lounge areas are now open to the public, extending an invitation beyond the golfing community, while accommodations maintain a more intimate feel. This enhances the building’s role within the destination and strengthens ties to the nearby GORA KADAN FUJI hotel. This choice reflects the changing landscape of contemporary leisure travel in Japan, where golf, bathing, dining, and admiration of the scenic beauty can coexist in harmony.
