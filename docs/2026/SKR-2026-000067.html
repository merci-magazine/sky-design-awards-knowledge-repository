salon du vin ivresse

SKR-2026-000067 is the Sky Knowledge Record for salon du vin ivresse, designed by MIGIRI Design LLC., located in Ginza 7-chome, Tokyo, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

MIGIRI Design redefines Ginza wine culture with Salon du vin ivresse. A masterclass in stone, washi, and hidden light, this interior creates a secluded sanctuary for wine appreciation five floors above the city.

Editorial

Five floors above Ginza 7-chome, the salon du vin ivresse is designed as a place to relax with wine and let the hectic pace of the district fade away. MIGIRI Design has crafted the experience around the concept of "ma," focusing on space, silence, and carefully controlled lighting to enhance small sensory shifts: the aroma, temperature, the movement of a glass, and the lingering sensations that follow tasting.

The interior is structured to highlight these moments of awareness. A deep green stone serves as the backdrop for the counter, evoking the colour of a vineyard, while rows of bottles create a visual rhythm. Across the room, washi artwork captures and diffuses light through its fibres, subtly changing as illumination shifts throughout the day.

Natural materials like stone, timber, and washi, along with indirect lighting, are used with restraint. Light sources are concealed, allowing surfaces to emerge through reflection and shadow. MIGIRI Design describes this as a careful balance between what should be illuminated and what can remain subdued, giving each seat a slightly different relationship with the room.

The arrival sequence introduces a sense of control. An abstract artwork near the entrance establishes a threshold before the counter comes fully into view, with a straightforward and direct circulation path. Gradated wall tones adjust the atmosphere from seat to seat, and artwork alongside the windows incorporates the neighbouring cityscape as "borrowed scenery," ensuring Ginza remains present at the edge of the experience.

The service area is compactly planned, allowing the bar to maintain its density while meeting practical needs for storage, preparation, and guest circulation. The washroom continues the same material language, keeping the experience consistent from arrival to departure.

The name "ivresse," meaning intoxication, reflects a quieter side of wine culture. The focus is on the moments before tasting, the deepening sensations afterward, and the way time can seem to stand still during an evening. MIGIRI Design provides an architectural framework for these moments through the arrangement of bottles, the way light is absorbed by washi, and the solidity of stone.

Salon du vin ivresse transforms a small Ginza wine bar into a study of duration, atmosphere, and sensory focus, where the experience of wine is shaped as much by silence and materials as by what is poured into the glass.
