CEVEN LAB

SKR-2026-000079 is the Sky Knowledge Record for CEVEN LAB, designed by NOMURA Co., Ltd., located in Nagano, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Discover CEVEN LAB by NOMURA Co., Ltd., an innovative sensory hub in Nagano where 300 years of spice tradition meets modern collaborative prototyping. This unique interior transformation redefines co-creation through live blending and sensory design.

Editorial

For almost three centuries, Yawataya Isogoro has turned spice into part of Nagano’s cultural identity. Founded in 1736, the company grew alongside the flow of pilgrims visiting Zenkoji, where hospitality was expressed through small acts of preparation, explanation and exchange. CEVEN LAB carries this tradition into a contemporary business setting, giving aroma and taste a central role in how new ideas are discussed and developed.

Created by NOMURA Co., Ltd. within a former confectionery workroom and warehouse, the facility brings together a test kitchen, showroom, archive, office, and business lounge. Its purpose responds to the way Yawataya Isogoro now works. The company has completed more than 300 collaborations and develops products from close to 40 spice ingredients, often with partners in food, fashion, entertainment, and popular culture. A request may begin with a brand story, a fictional character or an atmosphere that has yet to find a flavour. CEVEN LAB gives those abstract conversations a physical form. Partners can move directly from an image, word or cultural reference to ingredient selection, blending, aroma and tasting. The process places sensory experience at the centre of collaboration, allowing people from different industries to build a common language through something immediate and memorable.

The Blend Kitchen is where this exchange becomes visible. Two curved timber counters bring Yawataya Isogoro’s specialists and visiting partners together at the same height, with ingredients and samples passing easily across the shared surface. The arrangement creates the informality of a working studio and the concentration of a kitchen, giving each participant a direct role in shaping the result.

Above the counters, a circular golden canopy establishes a clear centre within the open warehouse. Its metallic surface recalls the lid of Yawataya Isogoro’s familiar shichimi tin, while integrated light draws attention to the activity below. The gesture connects brand memory with present-day work, giving a practical blending area the sense of occasion associated with a shared table. The wider interior keeps the character of the former warehouse visible. White-painted ducts, tracks and services remain overhead, set against pale timber joinery, light flooring and precise gold details. The designer, Ms. Yuri Nakagawa, uses this contrast to express a working culture that values both inherited knowledge and active experimentation. The atmosphere feels open, bright and purposeful, suited to a place where ideas need to move quickly between conversation and production.

Knowledge is made accessible through the archive. Shallow drawers hold past products, ingredients, and collaborations, while nearby displays showcase spices through colour, texture, and origin. A visitor can trace an earlier project, examine the materials behind it and carry that reference directly to the Blend Kitchen. The company’s history serves as a practical resource for new development, kept close to the people who use it. Seven coloured modular tables bring a more flexible rhythm to the collaboration area. Their tones refer to the components of shichimi, while their movable format supports small discussions, workshops and live-streamed sessions. A pair of collaborators can work beside the windows, then join a larger group as the project develops. The space adapts to the scale of the exchange, giving creative work room to expand.

This flexibility reflects a contemporary form of hospitality. Visitors are welcomed into the process through tasting, handling, and discussion, with Yawataya Isogoro’s staff sharing their knowledge up close. A spoonful of spice, a change in aroma or a small adjustment to a blend can communicate more clearly than a long presentation. The experience turns product development into a cultural encounter shaped by attention, curiosity and trust.

CEVEN LAB also brings Nagano’s food culture into wider creative conversations. Collaborations with brands and cultural properties introduce shichimi to audiences who may first encounter it through fashion, entertainment or design. The local ingredients and blending techniques remain visible throughout the process, giving each new partnership a direct connection to the company’s place of origin and accumulated expertise. By adapting an existing warehouse, NOMURA Co., Ltd. has created a workplace that supports research, business, and cultural exchange within a single shared environment. The project gives a long-standing regional company the means to work with new partners while keeping its knowledge active and tangible, but also presents heritage as something practised in the present. Yawataya Isogoro’s archive, ingredients and blending traditions provide the foundation, while the open kitchen and collaboration spaces invite new interpretations. The future of the brand is shaped by the same essential gestures that have defined it for generations: selecting, blending, tasting, and sharing.
