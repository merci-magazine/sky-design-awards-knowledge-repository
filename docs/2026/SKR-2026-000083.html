Editorial

In Zimo ● Songjing, light is integrated into the household as an essential element rather than just a finishing touch. NANJING SUKONG INTERIOR DESIGN CO. LTD. has transformed a traditional townhouse by introducing a new vertical sequence that allows daylight from a third-floor skylight to flow through the center of the home, connecting rooms that would otherwise feel like separate levels.

The transformation begins with the staircase, which has been reconfigured from two flights into three and paired with a transparent lift, making it the central feature of the residence. Daylight, movement, and glimpses of family life all pass through this vertical chamber, providing the house with a sense of internal orientation that is experienced from one floor to the next. The lift is an integral part of this architectural composition, carrying reflections and changing light throughout the day without obstructing the center of the layout.

The result is a home organized around relationships rather than isolated rooms. The basement, ground floor, and mezzanine support the more social aspects of daily life, while the upper levels are designated for bedrooms and private facilities. This gradual transition creates a natural rhythm throughout the house, moving from gathering and entertainment spaces to areas of rest and privacy, all while maintaining the visual continuity that unites the interior.

This continuity is particularly crucial in a multi-level home. While staircases typically function solely as a means of moving between floors, here they also allow family members to stay aware of activities happening elsewhere in the house. Sightlines across landings, openings that frame adjacent rooms, and light that travels vertically all contribute to a sense of connection within the domestic realm. The choice of materials is deliberately controlled. Light-colored surfaces reflect changing daylight, while darker cabinetry adds definition to the larger rooms. Warm red-brown tones introduce depth to the communal spaces. These elements establish continuity without flattening the individual character of each room. Living, dining, and transitional areas remain distinct yet are perceived as parts of a cohesive whole.

On the upper floor, the atmosphere becomes more personal. The third level is designed as a complete suite, where the architecture pays particular attention to the rituals of private life. An opening above the tatami area allows afternoon light to traverse textured walls, making the passage of time a visible component of the room. The master bedroom furthers this concept through its structure. Black beams and columns create a more intimate scale around the bed and incorporate the sloping roof into the design. Their presence stems from the structural renovation, giving the room a stronger architectural identity while allowing sleeping, storage, and tatami areas to coexist within one space.

Transparency is handled with equal thoughtfulness. Rippled glass blocks between the master bathroom and wardrobe allow light to permeate while maintaining privacy. In the south-facing bedroom, an acrylic screen separates sleeping and laundry functions by diffusion rather than complete enclosure. These materials keep adjacent activities connected through light, even when direct views are softened.

This approach gives the project relevance within contemporary residential design. Privacy is not merely viewed as a binary state of open or closed; instead, it can be modulated through translucency, distance, material choices, and varying levels of visibility. This same principle applies to the house as a whole: shared life remains perceivable without compromising the tranquil character of the upper floors.

NANJING SUKONG INTERIOR DESIGN CO. LTD. employs stairs, skylights, structural elements, and translucent boundaries to strengthen the relationship between everyday routines, light, and family interactions. The house becomes most expressive through the continually changing moments, such as a figure moving behind glass, afternoon sunlight crossing a wall, or an unexpected view opening between floors.
