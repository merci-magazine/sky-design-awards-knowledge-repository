JA KYOSAI SAITAMA BUILDING

SKR-2026-000073 is the Sky Knowledge Record for JA KYOSAI SAITAMA BUILDING, designed by MITSUI Designtec Co., Ltd., located in Saitama, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

JA KYOSAI SAITAMA BUILDING has undergone a common-area overhaul by MITSUI Designtec, which optimizes corporate workflow through lighting-driven design and flexible community zones within a 50-year-old structural framework.

Editorial

After more than fifty years of use, an office building develops its own unique habits. For the renovation of JA KYOSAI’s Saitama office, MITSUI Designtec focuses on these shared routines, transforming the common areas to herald a new era for an established workplace.

The project is described as “new traditional,” a phrase that resonates with an organization whose identity is rooted in continuity. MITSUI Designtec preserves the building’s sense of order and institutional presence while introducing spaces that reflect the increasingly fluid nature of office life. Formal meetings remain important, but so do the moments before and after them, as well as the need for informal workspaces away from the desk.

This transformation begins in the atrium. The double-height lobby maintains its composed and symmetrical design, yet timber accents, mirrored surfaces, and a tall illuminated feature bring a lighter rhythm to the existing space. Seating is integrated into the area, transforming arrival into a setting where people can comfortably wait, meet informally, or spend a few moments between engagements.

Lighting plays a crucial role throughout the renovation. At the entrance, a broad illuminated frame provides the building with a distinctive evening presence. Inside, light is skillfully used to enhance height, depth, and movement, bringing greater definition to spaces whose proportions were established decades ago. This approach allows the architecture to retain its familiarity while altering how it is perceived and used.

The third-floor conference area exemplifies one of the more contemporary aspects of the project. The space outside the meeting rooms is designed for collaborative use with communal tables and benches, recognizing that office activities often extend beyond formally bookable rooms. Preparation, follow-up discussions, and brief periods of individual work can occur in this area, thereby increasing the utility of a space previously primarily associated with circulation.

The relatively low ceiling becomes part of the interior character. Coffered sections, linear pendants, and indirect lighting draw attention along the length of the space, while timber and upholstered seating create a work lounge ambiance. The design responds to the existing architecture through proportion and lighting rather than extensive reconstruction.

The basement Community Plaza introduces a different pace to the workday. Featuring plantings, green tiles, timber, and a variety of seating options, this area serves as a setting for lunch, conversation, individual work, or a brief break away from the main office floors. An expanded-metal screen marks the entrance, offering glimpses of movement beyond while providing a degree of separation without isolating it from the rest of the building.

Across these spaces, furniture plays a significant role in effecting change. Sofas, counters, communal tables, and benches present different seating options and lengths of use, allowing employees to choose how and where they spend their time between formal tasks. This flexibility complements the more structured architectural framework of the existing office.

Additionally, there’s a broader workplace narrative in the decision to prioritize common spaces. Corporate interiors are often defined by desks, meeting rooms, and technology, but much of an organization’s culture is shaped elsewhere: at the entrance, around shared tables, and in the moments between scheduled meetings. MITSUI Designtec emphasizes these in-between moments, giving them a clearer role in JA KYOSAI’s daily life.

In essence, “new traditional” describes a dynamic relationship between continuity and contemporary usage. The Saitama office retains the presence of an institution that has been at this location for fifty years, while its shared spaces take on a more social and adaptable character. This renovation allows the building’s history to remain visible, even as the everyday working life continues to evolve around it.
