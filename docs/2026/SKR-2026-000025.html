Editorial Rewrite

PETSROCK occupies the ground floor of an industrial building in Hangzhou’s Grand Canal Hang Steel Park. Falab retains the robust character of the shell and fills it with a lighter, more agile system of yellow frames, timber boxes and movable seating. The result is a commercial interior organised equally around human routines and canine behaviour. The generous glazed perimeter keeps the park in view and gives dogs continuous visual contact with the exterior. Inside, the restaurant and retail floor remains broadly open. Seating modules are wider than a conventional single place, giving a dog room to settle beside its owner without becoming an obstacle in the aisle. Sliding elements allow the distance between groups to change as social conditions demand. Bright yellow mesh frames act as screens, display supports and attachment points. Their openness preserves sightlines between owners and animals, while orange-brown cushions bring warmth to the industrial palette of exposed red structure, grey service volumes and pale flooring. Timber counters and storage units introduce a second tactile register without disguising the building’s original character. The furniture is conceived as a set of replaceable parts. Metal, tile and plywood accommodate the harder wear expected in a pet environment; panels, seat backs and display pieces can be removed or renewed. Mobile trolleys, clipped accessories and modular shelving allow the operation to evolve without rebuilding the room. A long service core gathers the cashier, counter, kitchen and support spaces. Retail, pet-friendly dining, multifunctional display and activity areas occupy the public frontage, while storage, sterilisation, shipping and staff facilities sit behind. This clear separation keeps the commercial floor open and legible. PETSROCK is persuasive because its pet-centred claims are visible in ordinary details: leash points integrated into tables, low beds positioned beside seats, washable perforated surfaces, open sightlines and space for a dog to choose proximity or distance. Falab turns coexistence into a practical spatial system, framed by the memory of the industrial site.

Editor’s Review

The project advances beyond decorative pet branding. Its strongest decisions operate at the scale of behaviour: widened seating positions, adjustable separation, integrated leash points and clear visual connections. The open plan gives dogs and owners multiple ways to occupy the room, while the yellow mesh system establishes identity without producing solid barriers.

The retained red-painted structure and tall industrial glazing provide a convincing architectural backdrop. New elements are visibly lighter and reversible, which suits both the heritage setting and the submission’s maintenance strategy. The service core and back-of-house rooms are compactly arranged, leaving the public zone flexible. PETSROCK offers a credible prototype for commercial interiors where animal welfare is treated as a planning concern.

Drawing and Spatial Analysis

The plan places the main public entrance at the centre of the long glazed frontage. Retail occupies the central field; pet-friendly dining sits on both sides; multifunctional display and activity areas extend towards the perimeter. A second entrance at the right connects directly with the dining and activity zone. The cashier and counter form a hinge between the public floor and the central kitchen. Raw-material storage, sterilisation, shipping, staff changing, cold storage and food research rooms are consolidated across the rear and left side. This arrangement shortens service routes while protecting the open customer area. Broad aisles, low furniture and movable modules support changing social distances between dogs, owners and other visitors.
