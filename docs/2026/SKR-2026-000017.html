ABE galerie TOKYO

SKR-2026-000017 is the Sky Knowledge Record for ABE galerie TOKYO, designed by ODS / Oniki Design Studio, located in Shinjuku, Tokyo, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

ABE Galerie TOKYO by ODS (Oniki Design Studio) features artisan wooden doors and artwork within a plaster-lined office in Shinjuku. This gallery design emphasizes focus, creating a rhythmic architectural experience that showcases kumiko latticework and curated paintings.

Editorial

In celebration of its 80th anniversary, Abe’s Shinjuku office has collaborated with ODS / Oniki Design Studio to create a space that reflects a long-standing tradition of craftsmanship without reducing this history to a simple commemorative display. ABE Galerie TOKYO combines wooden doors featuring kumiko latticework, gold leaf, and urushi lacquer with paintings by artists with disabilities, establishing a cohesive environment where craftsmanship and artistic expression coexist.

The project, named ABE Galerie TOKYO by Oniki, focuses on directing visual attention. Five plaster-finished wing walls are arranged parallel to the façade, each rising elegantly toward the ceiling. This setup offers a sequential experience, allowing viewers to encounter the gallery piece by piece rather than all at once. Individual elements- a door, a painting, or a fragment of a nearby composition- reveal themselves in a manner that gives each its own moment within the space.

This intentional rhythm is vital for emphasizing workmanship. The precision needed for kumiko lattice, the depth of urushi layers, and the subtle variations in gold leaf based on light gain significance when given visual space. ODS ensures that the paintings hold equal importance, cultivating a relationship that goes beyond mere decoration. This strategy encourages a broader dialogue regarding techniques, the role of craftsmanship, and personal expression.

The architectural design is intentionally simple, which allows these distinctions to stand out. The plaster from the wing walls flows seamlessly onto the floor tiles, creating a cohesive material palette with a distinct hand-finished quality. This environment shares similarities with the objects it showcases: its value lies in the quality of execution and intricate details that invite careful observation.

At the end of the gallery, a mirrored surface extends the rhythm established by the five partitions, providing reflective views of previously encountered pieces. In this compact setting, the effect is about continuity rather than visual expansion. Earlier experiences resurface from different perspectives, and the movement through the space becomes essential to understanding the collection.

At ABE Galerie TOKYO, ODS has translated an 80-year history into a contemporary architectural language defined by focus, material sensitivity, and careful curation. The gallery does not lean on nostalgia for its significance; rather, its relevance comes from promoting direct relationships between various forms of craftsmanship and allowing the interior to engage meaningfully in this dialogue. The outcome is an environment where architecture acts as a subtle mediator among craft, art, and the core values that the organization strives to uphold.
