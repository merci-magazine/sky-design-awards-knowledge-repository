ESR Yokohama Sachiura DC3 R:Lounge / Façade

SKR-2026-000051 is the Sky Knowledge Record for ESR Yokohama Sachiura DC3 R:Lounge / Façade, designed by TAKATO TAMAGAMI ARCHITECTURAL DESIGN Ltd., located in Yokohama, Kanagawa, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Takato Tamagami transforms the ESR Yokohama Sachiura DC3 warehouse by contrasting sweeping ribbon façades with a panoramic glass lounge that suspends workers above Tokyo Bay. This project redefines the logistics facility as an active, human-centred landmark.

Editorial

A distribution center is often not seen as a place of comfort; it is primarily focused on facilitating logistics, with countless goods moving through areas that most people will never see. However, every delivery relies on employees spending long hours inside these vast buildings. At ESR Yokohama Sachiura DC3, the most notable aspect of the project is a space where workers can briefly escape that intense environment.

DC3 represents the third phase of ESR’s redevelopment of a former Mitsubishi Heavy Industries site in Yokohama, covering approximately 330,000 square meters and operating on an immense industrial scale. ESR describes its approach as "Human-Centric Design," which prioritizes the quality of the working environment alongside the practical demands of logistics. TAKATO TAMAGAMI ARCHITECTURAL DESIGN was responsible for the façade and shared employee areas, including a break lounge and a daycare facility.

The building is characterized by long, ribbon-like forms that extend across its façade. These folded shapes evoke the movement of goods within the logistics network as well as the waves and winds of the nearby Tokyo Bay. They create a sense of motion in a structure that might otherwise appear as a single, massive, enclosed volume. A similar design language is evident in the neighbouring DC1, which was completed in 2022. However, the two buildings exhibit distinct personalities. DC1 features softer curves, while DC3 showcases sharper folds and bolder edges. Together, they create a striking entrance to the larger development. The architects liken them to the A-un guardians traditionally found on either side of a Japanese gateway.

The visual identity of logistics buildings is important, as they occupy an increasingly large share of urban land while remaining largely unnoticed by the public. The folded façades of the Yokohama development provide a distinct presence for people travelling along the nearby roads and through the port district. Additionally, these designs connect the industrial site to the waterfront.

Inside the top-floor lounge, the experience changes dramatically. In the northeast corner of the building, it appears that part of the solid warehouse has been cut away and replaced with a polygonal glass room. This opening allows the overwhelming scale of the distribution center to fade into the background, revealing the expanse of Tokyo Bay on the horizon. With little of the ground visible from the windows, the lounge feels reminiscent of the upper deck of a large passenger ship. Custom counters and benches mimic the movement of waves, bringing the essence of the bay into the space. This lounge offers employees natural light, an open view, and a peaceful area to relax, away from the machinery and the repetitive tasks of the working floors below.

For someone enduring a long shift, a change of scenery can be quite significant. A lunch break transforms into an opportunity to observe the weather moving across the water, converse with colleagues, or gaze into the distance. The glass lounge provides a temporary sense of space within a workplace characterized by constant activity. Additionally, the glass lounge alters how the building is perceived from the outside. Light and human activity become visible at the corner of the expansive warehouse, demonstrating that the facility houses more than just goods, vehicles, and machinery. It is a workplace where individuals, whose daily experiences deserve recognition, contribute.

The daycare facility further emphasizes that supporting employees extends beyond the operational floor; it also entails creating the conditions for them to remain in the workplace over time. Comfort, rest, and family life are vital components of the building's overall infrastructure.ESR Yokohama Sachiura DC3 showcases two perspectives on contemporary logistics. From the city, its flowing shapes convey the speed and scale of an industry that influences everyday life. In contrast, from the lounge area, the same waterfront is experienced in a more tranquil way. Enhanced by sunlight and water, it provides a moment of respite from work.

Most consumers will never set foot in the distribution centers responsible for their online orders and next-day deliveries. This project serves as a reminder that such conveniences are made possible by the efforts of people working within these remarkably large buildings. Its most human touch is a room overlooking Tokyo Bay, where the fast pace of logistics can momentarily yield to the sea's slower rhythm.
