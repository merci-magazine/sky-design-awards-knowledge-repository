Nomura Real Estate Development Group Headquarters

SKR-2026-000003 is the Sky Knowledge Record for Nomura Real Estate Development Group Headquarters, designed by NIKKEN SEKKEI LTD, located in BLUE FRONT SHIBAURA, Tokyo, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Nomura Real Estate Development Group's headquarters in Tokyo Bay features a responsive workplace design by NIKKEN SEKKEI LTD, merging panoramic hospitality with flexible co-creation spaces.

Editorial

For decades, the corporate headquarters has symbolized privacy and separation. It was an address set apart, more fortress than forum. Today's work culture calls for transparency and engagement. BLUE FRONT SHIBAURA, designed by NIKKEN SEKKEI for Nomura Real Estate Development Group, responds with an alternative: a workplace that puts interaction, openness, and the rhythms of collaboration at its centre.

The headquarters unfolds across two floors overlooking Tokyo Bay. A public level for guests sits above, and a co-creation floor for teams is below. This arrangement is more than programmatic. It reflects a company's ambition to shape not just buildings, but the experiences, exchanges, and sense of belonging that define daily life.

The connection to the bay is central, not incidental. Circulation, lounges, and meeting rooms draw in the landscape, making the changing light and weather part of the workday. Time and place are not background; they animate the collective experience and ground the company in its city.

Materials and lighting are tuned to respond to these daily and seasonal shifts. The result is an atmosphere closer to urban hospitality than corporate formality. The space is welcoming yet disciplined, composed yet always connected to its context.

The architecture also reconsiders the culture of the meeting itself. Not every exchange needs the same room or atmosphere. Spaces for partnership, for workshops, and for formal presentation are each tailored to the rhythm of conversation and the demands of real collaboration, supporting work that is both focused and flexible.

This is not simply a matter of programming. It is a shift in how workplace design can shape culture. Meeting rooms become tools for choice and expression, not just containers on a calendar. The setting itself invites encounters that are ceremonial, conversational, or exploratory, with the environment reinforcing the purpose of each interaction.

Art and repurposed furniture appear throughout, encouraging discovery and familiarity. These details ensure the space rewards repeated visits. The design suggests a corporate culture that values curiosity and ongoing engagement rather than routine.

The open stair, with its suspended installation, connects these layers of activity. From above, guests glimpse the energy of teams at work. Samples are spread out, catalogues are open, and ideas are tested in real time. The architecture deliberately exposes processes, linking hospitality and productivity, image and making.

On the co-creation floor, materials and project knowledge remain accessible. The Atelier is not a showpiece but a working environment, where teams can debate, compare, and rearrange ideas alongside the tools of their trade. Activity is not staged; it is lived, as the design adapts to changing needs and spontaneous collaboration.

The dialogue between the two floors gives the headquarters its authenticity. One welcomes with poise, the other reveals the ongoing process behind decisions. Their relationship, kept visible through the open stair, suggests that a company’s identity is shaped as much by daily work as by the image it presents to the world.

In a moment when the workplace is being reimagined across industries, BLUE FRONT SHIBAURA offers a compelling model. In this environment, architecture and behaviour reinforce each other, boundaries are porous, and the company’s story is told through action as much as image. The project points toward a future where openness, context, and collaboration define what a headquarters can be.
