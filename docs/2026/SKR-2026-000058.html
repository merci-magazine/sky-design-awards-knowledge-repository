YAMAHA MUSIC YOKOHAMA MINATOMIRAI

SKR-2026-000058 is the Sky Knowledge Record for YAMAHA MUSIC YOKOHAMA MINATOMIRAI, designed by LCR, KOIZUMI Lighting Technology Corp., located in Yokohama, Kanagawa, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Yamaha Music Yokohama Minatomirai features a 100-meter lighting installation by Koizumi. The installation utilizes 1/f wave patterns to create a dynamic and rhythmic environment designed for an experiential music retail experience.

Editorial

At Yamaha Music Yokohama Minatomirai, lighting is essential for how the environment conveys music. The design, created by LCR, KOIZUMI Lighting Technology Corp., is inspired by the 1/f fluctuation concept, a pattern found in natural elements like ripples, wind, and starlight, where stability and variety coexist harmoniously.

This idea perfectly complements Yamaha’s philosophy. Music depends on rhythm, repetition, and pauses, yet it remains expressive because these aspects are never entirely mechanical. The lighting in this venue reflects that principle, establishing a visual tempo that can be sensed even before any instrument is played.

A sequence of white louvers extends roughly 100 meters throughout the interior, above the music lounge, café, and other public spaces. Their curved shapes create an uninterrupted flow across this large commercial area while allowing enough depth for a more complex lighting arrangement.

The downlights are carefully placed based on overlapping waveforms that stem from natural 1/f rhythms. Their spacing subtly varies across the ceiling, resulting in a composition that feels unified without adhering to a conventional retail design. Viewed from below, the lights appear like scattered musical notes instead of simply repeated fixtures.

Beneath this visual simplicity lies significant technical complexity. The team modelled the ceiling and the placement of lights in three dimensions, testing the rhythm against the practical requirements for instrument displays, movement, seating, and visual comfort. Modifications were made whenever the spatial design required greater accuracy. This interaction between calculation and design intuition imparts character to the project. The numerical framework establishes the rhythm’s foundation, while the placement of lights reacts to how people navigate through the area and interact with the instruments.

In the music lounge, curved seating arrangements position instruments conveniently, fostering a more casual relationship with sound. A large digital artwork acts as a visual centrepiece, while the ceiling preserves the same fluctuating rhythm overhead. The café mirrors this design language through tables and counters, establishing a cohesive visual identity for both hospitality and music retail.

The illuminated ceiling also enhances the building's visibility at night. Through the glazed façade of Yokohama Symphostage, the long line of louvers and dispersed light gives Yamaha Music a unique prominence from the street, connecting the activities inside with the larger Minatomirai district.

At Yamaha Music Yokohama Minatomirai, the 1/f principle shifts from scientific observation to an everyday cultural context. LCR, KOIZUMI Lighting Technology Corp. employs it to shape the ceiling's rhythm, light distribution, and how visitors engage with music, gathering, and exploration within the same environment.
