Park Homes Shijo Kawaramachi

SKR-2026-000068 is the Sky Knowledge Record for Park Homes Shijo Kawaramachi, designed by Mitsui Designtec Co., Ltd., located in Kyoto, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

Park Homes Shijo Kawaramachi blends 400 years of temple heritage with contemporary residential design. Mitsui Designtec incorporates Nishijin-ori textiles, Kyoto's garden culture, and grid-based architecture to create a functional gallery for daily communal living.

Editorial

At Park Homes Shijo Kawaramachi, Mitsui Designtec draws inspiration from the rich cultural fabric of Kyoto to shape the communal experience of a modern residence. The building is located next to a temple with a history spanning four centuries, and its shared interiors incorporate elements of Nishijin weaving, seasonal flowers, literature, garden culture, and the city's historic street grid.

The communal areas are designed as a private “Gallery of Kyoto.” Art, crafts, and local references are integrated into the residents’ daily pathways through the building, giving the common spaces a cultural identity that evolves over time with repeated use.

A striking glass art installation marks the double-height entrance. This piece incorporates ro, the open gauze traditionally used for summer kimonos, and transforms its lightness into a suspended composition of fibre, glass, and gold. Viewed from the entrance, staircase, and upper landing, the installation changes with different perspectives and light conditions, bringing a sense of movement to the atrium through shifting transparency and reflection.

Stone, dark metal, and timber create a subtle backdrop for this artwork. Beneath the stairs, rocks and gravel form a dry landscape that connects the entrance to Kyoto’s temple and garden traditions through both material choice and composition. On the second floor, the communal program is organized around three themes: IKOU, TSUTAU, and MANABU, which translate to relax, inherit, and learn. IKOU features a lounge space for conversation and relaxation. TSUTAU showcases contemporary Nishijin-ori and local crafts. MANABU provides an environment for reading that includes books, ceramics, and references to Kyoto’s cultural history.

Nishijin-ori also influences the spatial layout. The interplay of warp and weft is reflected in lattice screens that filter views between connected areas. Their layered design allows light and movement to flow across the floor while creating a clear sense of enclosure for each space. Kyoto’s goban-no-me, the historic grid that has shaped the city for centuries, is echoed in the alignment of floors, shelves, and partitions within the interior. This consistent geometry allows the unique characteristics of stone, timber, textiles, and artwork to shine through.

MANABU organizes books, ceramics, and artworks in a tranquil setting suited for reading and close observation, while IKOU offers a more residential atmosphere. Loose seating, natural light, and floral arrangements make it a relaxed space for residents to unwind outside their apartments. Seasonality is also a significant consideration. Flowers and branches are incorporated into the lattice and along circulation routes, reflecting Kyoto’s longstanding tradition of marking the seasons with thoughtfully chosen arrangements.

The project also embraces okuyukashisa, a sensibility associated with discretion, depth, and refinement. Artworks, objects, and materials can be discovered from various distances and through layered views, providing a sense of exploration that unfolds over time. Park Homes Shijo Kawaramachi integrates Kyoto’s craft traditions, literature, and seasonal culture into contemporary residential life. Mitsui Designtec weaves these references into the daily experiences of the building, establishing a strong connection between its shared spaces and the cultural essence of the city.
