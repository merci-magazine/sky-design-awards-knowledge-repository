Editorial Rewrite

Airports function as hubs of constant transition, facilitating the arrival and departure of numerous individuals who engage in activities such as waiting, shopping, dining, meeting, transferring, and preparing for subsequent stages of their journeys, often within the same few hours. At Fukuoka International Airport, SEMBA Corporation approaches the renewal of the terminal by considering this entire cycle of movement, expanding capacity while enhancing the passenger experience's connection to Fukuoka and the Kyushu region.

By the year 2018, the number of international passengers had increased to approximately 2.9 times the level recorded at the terminal's opening in 1999. This considerable growth exerted pressure on departure and arrival facilities, retail areas, services, and waiting spaces. The renewal initiative seeks to address this growth by posing a critical question: how can an international terminal maintain operational efficiency while simultaneously providing travellers with a pronounced sense of place?

The response to this inquiry manifests differently throughout the airport. In the pre-departure commercial areas, design elements inspired by Yame lanterns and festival floats have been transformed into large architectural installations, creating strong visual landmarks. These features are intended to be readily recognizable within the expansive concourse, accommodating the rapid pace of passenger movement among shopping, dining, security, and boarding gates.

Conversely, the quieter lounges incorporate regional crafts such as Hakata dolls, Okawa kumiko, Hakata textiles, and Koishiwara pottery. These crafts are integrated into furniture, screens, decorative elements, and washbasins, enriching the ordinary moments of waiting and rest. Passengers are thus afforded the opportunity to appreciate the materials, textures, and finer details of these local crafts.

The food and retail areas within the airport offer a more dynamic representation of local culture. Timber structures, lanterns, graphics, and references to Hakata street life contribute to a vibrant social atmosphere, effectively transforming dining and shopping into integral components of the terminal experience rather than merely secondary activities between flights.

Additionally, small tsuboniwa gardens introduce a serene rhythm to the airport environment. The gardens, featuring stone, moss, and plantings, provide visual pauses and moments of tranquillity amidst the more demanding phases of travel. Their presence also brings a sense of seasonality to an otherwise continuously operating environment.

The arrival spaces are characterized by clarity and restraint. A continuous ceiling feature crafted from locally sourced timber establishes a strong directional flow around information points, transport connections, and onward movement. While this material adds warmth, its primary function is to organize various routes at critical points, helping passengers quickly comprehend the terminal's layout.

This deliberate variation in design across departure, waiting, retail, dining, and arrival areas contributes to the overall character of the renewal project. SEMBA opts against applying a single visual identity uniformly throughout the terminal; rather, cultural references are adjusted in scale and intensity according to the duration of passenger stay, movement pace, and engagement in particular activities.

At Fukuoka International Airport, regional identity is intricately integrated into the practical travel experience. Passengers encounter regional crafts at close range, while festival culture leaves a noticeable impression on the larger concourses. Gardens create spaces for respite, and the use of local timber enhances spatial clarity. This comprehensive design approach serves both directions of travel, ensuring that Fukuoka is present from initial check-in and departure through arrival and onward travel.

Drawing and Spatial Analysis

The diagrams illustrate the terminal's organization around three distinct occupancy levels. The first floor (1F) arrival lobby focuses on immediate passenger orientation and movement, while the third floor (3F) duty-free area allows for a slower, exploratory circulation, integrating retail and dining options. The fourth floor (4F) lounge is designed for prolonged stays, ensuring that a singular Fukuoka-themed interior is not uniformly applied throughout the airport.

On 1F, the design transforms the arrival hall into a cohesive central axis, with the Welcome Gate directly in front of passengers and symmetrically placed curved service counters. The timber ceiling enhances this layout, guiding passengers toward buses and transportation connections.

In contrast, the third floor features a curving circulation area inspired by the Naka River, leading passengers through diverse sections for fashion, cosmetics, and the Hakata Food Hall. The Symbol Tower, situated at the convergence of these areas, serves as a visible reference point, integrating cultural allusions to festivals and yatai.

On the fourth floor, the focus shifts to the experience of occupation, with seating arranged along windows that create intimate spaces for relaxation, conversation, and work. Elements such as Kumiko, Hakata weaving, and pottery enrich the atmosphere, fostering longer engagement from passengers.

The drawings reflect three spatial progressions: structured arrival, distributed departure, and intimate lounge environments. The varying scales of regional references—from a prominent timber canopy to an urban Symbol Tower and crafted details—illustrate the dynamic relationship between passenger behaviour, spatial organization, and cultural expression central to the design. large, directional timber canopy to an urban-scale Symbol Tower, down to crafted elements experienced at the scale of the hand and furniture. This relationship between passenger behaviour, spatial organization, and cultural expression serves as the central design logic of the project.

Original Designer Submission — Verbatim

SKY Design Awards Fukuoka International Airport Project description

We renewed the Fukuoka Airport International Terminal under the concept of creating “an airport that becomes a destination in itself, filled with moments of surprise and delight.” By enabling visitors to experience the culture and four seasons of Japan and Fukuoka within a dynamic airport environment, we transformed the terminal from a mere transportation facility into a new tourism asset that contributes to regional economic and cultural vitality.

Since its opening in 1999, passenger numbers at Fukuoka International Airport had grown rapidly—reaching approximately 2.9 times the original volume by 2018—resulting in serious issues such as congestion, insufficient departure and arrival functions, and a shortage of retail and service areas. In response, we reimagined the previously inorganic and cramped terminal as both the place where travelers first encounter Japan and the “final place” where they linger in the afterglow of their journey, creating a space that would remain in visitors’ memories.

We focused particularly on showcasing the exquisite techniques of Japanese traditional crafts and their evolution into contemporary expressions. Our goal was to create a new platform within the airport that supports the inheritance and development of local craftsmanship for people arriving from all over the world. This vision is most clearly embodied in the lounge area. Beginning with a welcoming display of Hakata dolls, we integrated wall features using Okawa kumiko wood lattice and Hakata textiles, as well as washbasins crafted from Koishiwara pottery—placing representative crafts of Fukuoka throughout the space. In the pre-departure area, we installed structures inspired by Yame lanterns and festival floats, reconstructing traditional crafts at an airport scale. By doing so, we infused the charm of tradition with dynamism and playfulness unique to an airport environment, translating heritage into expressions that resonate with today’s travelers.

In the arrival lobby, we created a space where efficient transportation functions and visual beauty complement each other. Even in a facility where complex circulation paths intersect, we achieved intuitive wayfinding by extending a striking ceiling feature made from locally sourced wood continuously from the arrival lobby through to the guidance areas, naturally leading passengers without confusion.

Beyond simply expanding floor area and increasing dining and retail capacity, we realized a coherent cultural experience from the beginning of the journey to the final moments before departure. Across the pre-departure zone, lounges, concourses, and arrival lobby, visitors can consistently experience the essence of Japan and Fukuoka. We introduced elements such as Tsuboniwa courtyard gardens reflecting the changing seasons, craft-based installations, and designs evoking local festivals and landscapes—creating a terminal that balances cultural immersion with comfort and dwell time and elevates the airport into a memorable destination.
