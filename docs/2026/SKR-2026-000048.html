AMAMI HOUSE

SKR-2026-000048 is the Sky Knowledge Record for AMAMI HOUSE, designed by SAKAI ARCHITECTS / Kazunori Sakai, located in Amami Ōshima, Kagoshima, Japan. The project is shortlisted / finalist for Sky Design Awards 2026.

Project summary

AMAMI HOUSE brings off-grid resilience and Amami Ōshima’s communal traditions together in a five-volume family home shaped by climate, circular living and connection.

Editorial

On Amami Ōshima, Kazunori Sakai has used his own family home to test a demanding proposition: can a house for four operate without grid electricity or air conditioning in a humid subtropical climate with limited solar yield? AMAMI HOUSE answers through vernacular intelligence, resource cycles and a social life as important as its energy performance.

Five separate volumes reinterpret the island’s buntō tradition, assigning functions such as sleeping, bathing and storage to distinct roofs. The spaces between them become the home’s generous shared rooms, extending towards verandas and gardens. Corrugated metal and irimoya-like profiles, deep eaves, layered insulation, raised construction and cross-ventilation temper sun, wind and sudden rain.

Self-sufficiency is embedded in daily routines. Construction offcuts fuel a wood-fired sauna; kitchen waste returns as compost; and garden produce comes back to the table. These are modest loops, but together they make consumption visible to the household and reduce dependence on distant systems.

The house remains open to the gatherings that define Amami as an “Island of Ties”. Sakai connects environmental autonomy with yui, the culture of mutual cooperation: resilience here does not mean retreating from neighbours, but creating a place capable of supporting them.
