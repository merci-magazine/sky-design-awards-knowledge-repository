Muneji Toh Architects transforms industrial perforated sheet metal into dynamic pendant lighting using metal-spinning techniques. This process stretches the standard grid into custom geometries that project intricate, shifting light patterns across interior spaces.

## Editorial Rewrite

The Perforated Metal Spinning Lamp by Muneji Toh Architects is shaped by a small but consequential decision: to spin a sheet of metal after it has already been perforated.

Metal spinning is normally associated with smooth, controlled surfaces. In this case, the regular grid of openings is subjected to the same forming process as the metal itself. As the sheet is drawn into a three-dimensional shape, the perforations stretch with it, changing proportion according to their position on the curve. What begins as an industrially ordered surface becomes progressively less uniform as the form develops.

That change gives the lamp much of its character. The pattern isn't merely decorative surface ornament; it is the visible consequence of fabrication. The finished surface records how the material has moved, with the original perforation grid still legible beneath the distortions created by spinning.

When illuminated, those deformations become spatial. Light passes through openings of different shapes and projects an uneven field onto surrounding walls, making the effect of the process visible beyond the object itself. The shade therefore operates at two scales: as a compact pendant and as a source of changing light and shadow within the room.

There is also something useful in the tension between repetition and variation. The starting material is systematic, while the forming process introduces differences. Neither condition disappears completely. The lamp retains the discipline of an industrial product while allowing the behaviour of the material and the skill of the maker to remain visible.

For Muneji Toh Architects, the significance resides in this precise interplay between technique and form. Unburdened by extraneous motifs, the object relies entirely on its constituent act of making unifying structure, perforation, and illumination into a direct expression of industrial craft.

## Editor’s Review

This piece effectively combines industrial processes with striking visual results. By using the metal spinning technique on perforated metal, the design transforms a standard grid into a dynamic, three-dimensional tapestry. This approach provides a genuine narrative of its creation, as the deformation of the perforations showcases the artisan's skill. The pendant serves a dual purpose: it functions as a primary task light while also casting an ambient, patterned projection in its surroundings. It exemplifies how precise manipulation of raw materials can result in functional art.

## Original Designer Submission — Verbatim

We created a lamp using metal spinning. Metal spinning is a processing technique that uses a special machine to transform a sheet of metal into a 3 dimensional shape. We applied this metal spinning technology to the lighting fixtures.

Normally, metal plates are used, but the highly skilled craftsmen add a spinning process to perforated metal plates. The spinning process stretches the perforated metal, changing the regular perforation pattern to create special patterns of light and shadow.
