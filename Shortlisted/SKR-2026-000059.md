EDITORIAL REWRITE                                                         						     Club Anonymous represents a departure from conventional members' clubs, particularly within the context of Hong Kong, where exclusivity is often signified through visible signage and elaborate ceremonies. Adapa Architects Limited has strategically eliminated nearly all external indicators of the club. The entrance features an unmarked black steel door, leading to a narrow vaulted passage and a singular circular opening. This design choice establishes an initial distance, allowing the bustling city outside to recede into the background. This decision is significant as the 6,000-square-foot interior is constructed around a distinctive interpretation of privacy. Membership is not indicated by grand reception areas or formal processions; rather, the club is organized around varying degrees of participation. This arrangement enables individuals to transition smoothly between activities such as conversation, retreat, dining, and events, without the constraints of rigid spatial definitions for each setting. The main living room immediately exemplifies this principle of freedom. A broad modular sofa occupies the central area, creating an adaptable social landscape, complemented by a bar that shares the same visual field. Exposed concrete beams, unfinished textures, and precisely crafted arches prevent the room from appearing overly polished, while a delicate glass chandelier introduces an element of fragility overhead. This contrast imbues the space with character without rendering it contrived. The informality inherent in this design is central to the attraction of the club. Members may arrive for a drink, linger at the bar, engage in larger conversations, or settle into one of the sofa groupings. The architectural design does not prescribe specific uses for the room; instead, it allows interpersonal relationships to dictate the utilization of the space. The Sun Lounge introduces a marked change in atmosphere. With travertine flooring, reclaimed timber, deeper upholstery, and a central fireplace, it cultivates a warmer, more domestic ambiance. The adjoining terrace enhances this sense of relaxation, making the space suitable for both leisurely afternoons and intimate conversations without completely detaching members from the club’s social environment. In contrast, the private dining room sharpens the overall mood. Its black surfaces, antique mirrored ceiling, and concentrated lighting create a deliberately ceremonial atmosphere around the table. This impact is heightened by the contrast with the adjacent spaces, as the club intentionally avoids a uniform aesthetic. Instead, it employs varying atmospheres to facilitate different modes of engagement. An internal courtyard adds further complexity to this social structure. An artificial skylight ensures even illumination for the plant life and patterned stone, while function rooms and a cooking studio open toward it. What may have been merely a circulation area takes on its own identity, serving as a venue where diverse activities can intersect. Events can extend into this space, conversations can persist, and individuals moving between rooms remain connected to the broader club environment. Consistent features such as repeated arches, circular openings, and framed views reinforce a sense of gradual revelation. Rooms are seldom fully visible at first glance; instead, their edges become perceptible first, followed by glimpses of light, furnishings, or other groups beyond. Privacy, therefore, is managed through spatial distance, angles, and partial visibility, rather than solely by means of closed doors. Club Anonymous serves as a compelling contemporary membership culture. Its luxury is not embedded in ostentation or material wealth but in the empowerment of individuals to control their visibility, sociability, or withdrawal in any given moment. Adapa Architects effectively transforms the concept of anonymity into a spatial principle, featuring a discreet entrance, a progression of changing moods, and a club environment where privacy is delineated not merely by separation, but by individual choice.

Discover 'Primitive Luxury' at TAIZA AWAI-CLUB SHIROSHIMA VILLA by supermaniac inc. This exclusive Japanese ryokan retreat blends minimalist architecture with the raw beauty of the Kyoto sea. Behind an unmarked Hong Kong doorway, Adapa Architects composes a private club as a sequence of raw rooms, luminous salons and sheltered corners calibrated for different degrees of company.

## Editorial Rewrite

Shiroshima is a small island off the coast of Taiza in Kyotango, separated from the mainland by water and a narrow passage. This island has long been considered a sacred place, imbuing it with a rich cultural and spiritual significance. Any hospitality project established here must be designed with sensitivity to the landscape and aim to provide guests with a meaningful experience.

TAIZA AWAI-CLUB SHIROSHIMA VILLA consists of two accommodation buildings, Mizu Yadoru and Hi Yadoru, designed by Nobuaki Suzuki of supermaniac inc. Together, these buildings exemplify the concept of "Primitive Luxury," focusing on time, privacy, and a close relationship with nature. This evolving interpretation of luxury highlights the contemporary traveller's desire to slow down, revel in privacy, and engage mindfully with their surroundings. The rhythm of a stay at Shiroshima is dictated by the sea, weather, and changing daylight, allowing each guest's experience to resonate with the island itself.

In the architectural design, supermaniac inc. minimized the use of walls and columns to enhance connectivity to the environment, fulfilling privacy needs while maintaining a bond with nature. This strategy enables light, sea air, and the sound of water to flow freely throughout the interior spaces, allowing guests to stay linked to the island experience even indoors.

Mizu Yadoru is designed with an openness toward the sea, while Hi Yadoru offers a more sheltered atmosphere that promotes contemplation. This diversity in design allows guests to engage with the island through varying levels of light, enclosure, and view. Rooms in Mizu Yadoru provide a horizon view just steps from the bed. Timber latticework and shoji-like screens filter daylight, creating a seamless transition between the interior and exterior. The ambiance within shifts naturally throughout the day, as sunlight travels across the space. A prominent treasure-wheel motif behind the bed symbolizes gratitude to the ocean, reflecting Taiza's fishing heritage and serving as a visual focal point. Its natural texture complements the room’s overall material palette.

Conversely, Hi Yadoru promotes a more introspective experience, utilizing darker tones and exposed timber to enhance the sense of shelter. This design encourages guests to immerse themselves in the island's textures and subtleties before shifting their focus to the expansive landscape beyond. The contrast between the two buildings adds emotional depth to the retreat: Mizu Yadoru directs attention to the horizon and the dynamic sea, while Hi Yadoru envelops guests in tranquillity and safety. Both structures uphold an appreciation for natural materials and the island's unique character.

Shared living spaces feature low furniture that maintains a visual connection with the coastline. In a sunken seating area, guests gather around a low table, enjoying uninterrupted views of pine trees, rocks, and the open sea. This setting invites conversation, reflection, and a deeper engagement with the surrounding environment.

The dining area fosters a more intimate connection with the landscape. A long timber table sits beneath striking dark pendant lights, complemented by a glazed wall that opens outward. As day transitions to night, views shift from the sea's brightness to the island's rich hues and shadows. Timber, earthy plaster, woven screens, stone, and dark metals create a warm and textured interior, providing a stable refuge against the ever-changing conditions outside. These materials allow the elements- weather, season, and natural light to weave into the character of each room.

The understated scale of the buildings respects the interplay between architecture and landscape. Dark timber walls, sloped roofs, and carefully curated stone gardens allow the villas to integrate harmoniously among the existing pines. Aerial views reveal the coastline, rock formations, and tree canopy as defining features of Shiroshima. "Primitive Luxury" encapsulates an experience shaped by time, place, and sensory attentiveness. At the villa, comfort resides in privacy, silence, natural materials, and the unhurried observation of the sea. The architecture creates an environment in which these elements can be fully appreciated throughout the day.

TAIZA AWAI-CLUB SHIROSHIMA VILLA offers guests a unique and respectful connection to one of Kyotango's coastal landscapes. Through Mizu Yadoru and Hi Yadoru, supermaniac inc. has created two complementary spaces that embody this vision.

## Editor’s Review

The project explores a masterful discipline of subtraction, where every structural choice exists to give the coastline and sacred landscape room to breathe. The interiors serve as a vessel for light, air, and the rhythms of the sea, inviting the environment into the guest's daily rituals. Mizu Yadoru and Hi Yadoru offer a sophisticated duality: the former acts as a luminous, expansive connection to the horizon, while the latter serves as a grounded, introspective sanctuary. Luxury here finds expression in the restoration of time and the heightening of sensory awareness. Symbolic elements, such as the treasure-wheel motif, are woven into the architecture with restraint, feeling indigenous to the space and honouring the site's heritage with elegance.

The architectural design focuses on the sequence of movement within the space. A narrow entry leads to a spacious main lounge, where changes in lighting and material textures guide members through the area. The club operates as a series of thresholds that gradually reveal the interior.

Confidence characterizes the use of contrasting materials. Exposed concrete provides a strong foundation, complemented by the elegance of glass chandeliers and marble arches. Travertine and reclaimed timber add warmth, while antique mirrors create depth in the dining areas. Each colour palette encourages a distinct way of engaging with the space.

The courtyard serves as both a social and structural centrepiece. It establishes a bright focal point within a deep floor plan, offering an informal meeting area for the surrounding activity rooms. This design transforms the spatial configuration into the central connective vein of the club.

## Peggy’s Editor’s Note

Club Anonymous redefines membership culture by prioritizing social agency. The architecture offers a wide range of engagement options, empowering members to navigate the space with complete freedom. The design accommodates various interpersonal rhythms, allowing individuals to create their own social experiences.

A harmonious blend of glamour and comfort is evident throughout the space. A chandelier hangs beneath raw concrete, candlelight warms the travertine, and greenery thrives beneath an artificial sky, showcasing a thoughtful balance. These contrasts give each room a unique personality. Exclusivity manifests as a quiet privilege, enabling members to move at their own pace.

## Drawing and Spatial Analysis

No architectural plan or section was supplied in the reviewed project package. The spatial reading is therefore reconstructed from the complete ten-image set and the designer’s room-by-room description, and should be checked against drawings if they are later provided.

The evidence indicates a sequential plan organised by degrees of exposure. The unmarked entrance leads through a compressed vaulted passage into the large living room and bar. The Sun Lounge and private dining room form adjacent but atmospherically distinct destinations, while the internal courtyard sits among function rooms and the cooking studio as a shared centre.

Repeated arches and framed openings maintain visual continuity without making every room fully visible. This lets the club combine orientation with discretion: members can read where activity is occurring, then choose whether to join, pass through or withdraw.

“Primitive Luxury” evolves into a profound philosophy of place at Shiroshima Villa. The project treats the landscape with absolute reverence, creating an architectural dialogue where every decision amplifies the island’s natural beauty. The innovation of the villa lies in its capacity to curate a guest’s experience around the quiet, unfolding narratives of weather, sea, and light. It offers a compelling vision of contemporary hospitality, centred on fostering a state of profound presence. In this setting, the most meaningful amenities such as silence, privacy, and the unhurried observation of nature’s enduring cycles become the primary focus, offering guests an immersive connection to the island's rhythms.

No architectural plans or sections were supplied in the project folder. The spatial reading is therefore based on the eight photographs and the presentation panel. These materials indicate two separate, low-rise ryokan buildings distributed within the island landscape, with private rooms, shared lounges and dining spaces oriented towards different coastal conditions.

Interior organization is based on layered thresholds. Timber lattice, sliding screens and changes in floor level separate sleeping, lounge and circulation zones without relying on heavy partitions. Sea-facing rooms use controlled openings and low furniture to protect privacy while maintaining a continuous relationship with the horizon.

The shared rooms appear to occupy the most open edges of the buildings. Sunken seating, glazed corners and low tables place occupation close to the ground, while the dining room turns towards vegetation and night views. Externally, the buildings maintain a compact footprint among existing pines and stone, preserving the island’s larger visual and ecological structure.

Company URL: https://supermaniac.ne.jp/

Design: Nobuaki Suzuki (supermaniac inc.)

A ryokan that embraces nature and converses with it

Nestled within Taiza, Kyotango, the two buildings of “Mizu Yadoru” and “Hi Yadoru”, the Shiroshima Villa of Sumihei Taiza Awai-Club, occupy the entire island of Shiroshima. This island, long revered as a sacred dwelling place, stands solitary and secluded. The concept is “Primitive Luxury.” Believing that true luxury lies in embracing nature as it is and conversing with it, the design prioritized removing walls and pillars wherever possible. This allows not only the natural scenery but even the very essence of nature to permeate the space. The luxury here lies in simply watching and listening to the sea spread before you, letting time pass. Design progressed with the priority of accentuating this luxury.

True to its ancient name, “Kami Yadoru Island” a sacred dwelling place, this place exudes a profoundly positive energy. Out of respect for the island, the design strives to preserve nature as much as possible. In the rooms of “Mizu Yadoru” facing the sea, a large treasure wheel adorns the headboard, expressing reverence for the ocean. In this way, gratitude for being allowed to spend time with the island is woven into every detail.
