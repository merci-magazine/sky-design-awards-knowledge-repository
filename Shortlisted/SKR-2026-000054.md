## Editorial

Tokyo has long been a city where permanence is measured less by age than by meaning. Buildings are altered, replaced and absorbed into the next chapter of urban life, while the memories attached to them continue to shape how the city is understood. Urban House in Tokyo enters this condition with quiet confidence. RON DESIGN and Articious Co., Ltd. have renovated a 40-year-old reinforced-concrete residence into a home for someone whose daily life spans countries, cultures, and time zones, creating a place that offers consistency without feeling fixed.
The project is rooted in a distinctly contemporary idea of home. For its owner, Tokyo is both a point of return and one city within a wider personal geography. The design does not reproduce the visual language of Paris, New York or any other familiar destination. Travel is present through memory, mood and the accumulated culture of objects. A piece of furniture, the quality of light or the intimacy of a late-night bar may call another city to mind, yet these associations remain personal and unforced. The house becomes a setting in which different places can coexist through experience.
This sensibility is supported by a careful reworking of the existing architecture. The design team removed layers of compartmentalization and read the plan, section, structure and building services as a single composition. On the upper floor, former bedrooms and a bathroom were opened to form a generous living, dining and kitchen space. Light and views now define the house's social life, while the exposed ceiling volume gives the original concrete frame a clearer presence. Services were coordinated around the existing beams with precision, allowing technical requirements to sit comfortably within the architectural language.
As the house descends, its tempo slows and becomes more private. Four bedrooms and associated facilities accommodate the changing patterns of daily life, from solitary routines to visits from friends and guests. Furniture, lighting and textiles sourced in Japan and overseas bring another layer of cultural exchange, selected not as decoration but in response to movement, use and atmosphere. In the basement, a bar and sleeping area create a more intimate world shaped by low light, tactile surfaces and the social rituals of conversation, music and retreat.
Urban House in Tokyo is ultimately a study of how architecture can hold a life that is always moving. Its value lies not only in preserving an existing concrete structure but also in how the design reconnects people with place. The house carries the density of Tokyo, the cultural impressions of travel, and the comfort of familiar objects within a single continuous interior landscape. It is a home designed for departure and return, grounded in the city while remaining open to the world.
