## Excerpt

Witness the mechanics behind Japanese model-making. CIC, NOMURA Co., Ltd. turn a working Bandai factory into a public laboratory, blending live production lines with a 4,000-piece fan gallery. See how industrial engineering meets creative design in this ZEB-certified manufacturing showcase.

## Editorial

Plastic models are not just toys; they embody a profound cultural phenomenon in Japan. These creations begin as industrial products but become deeply personal expressions in the hands of builders who cut, assemble, paint, and reinterpret them with ingenuity and patience. The Bandai Hobby Center takes this concept to new heights with its PLAMO DESIGN INDUSTRIAL INSTITUTE MUSEUM, a space that elegantly intertwines the artistry of model-making with the very factory where these iconic pieces are born.
Crafted by CIC, NOMURA Co., Ltd., this museum offers visitors a captivating journey from concept to creation. It immerses them in the intricacies of production, revealing moulds, forming machines, runners, and production tools that showcase the precision and engineering behind the models they admire. This experience redefines plastic modelling as a harmonious blend of engineering, design, and individual creativity.
The museum's innovative setting enhances this understanding by featuring an operational factory. Visitors can witness the scale, machinery, and disciplined processes of manufacturing, lending authenticity to a narrative that often remains hidden behind the polished final product. A striking full-scale plastic model façade invites guests into this evocative space, communicating in a language they instinctively grasp. The enlarged panels, joints, and assembly lines transpose model kit logic into architectural form. What may initially seem playful also sharpens the eye to observe how components connect, how surfaces interact, and how construction influences overall form.
Inside, authentic moulding equipment and runner formations demystify the production sequence, guiding visitors through the transformational journey from creative concepts to precise metal dies and reproducible components. The museum artfully bridges the worlds of design and manufacturing, making every step of this evolution visible. Engagement is key to this experience. Guests can step into the shoes of a plastic model designer, tasked with applying the insights and ideas they've encountered throughout the exhibition. This shift from passive observation to active creation reinforces the dynamic relationship between technical expertise and imaginative expression.
For younger visitors, the familiarity of plastic models serves as a direct gateway into the world of manufacturing culture. Through these objects linked to play and popular culture, they can grasp concepts of precision, material behaviour, and planning. The museum is a nurturing ground for curiosity, inviting exploration, comparison, and experimentation. A highlight of the exhibit is the stunning installation of 4,000 models crafted by enthusiasts, a powerful testament to the diverse interpretations within the fan community. Each model is a unique reflection of its builder's choices, underscoring how a standardized product can inspire a multitude of personal expressions once it leaves the factory.


This intricate dialogue between producers and users gives the museum its unique essence. Bandai supplies the engineering, components, and systems, while builders contribute their time, skill, and individuality. Together, they create a shared culture that celebrates plastic models.
Complementing this narrative are the factory’s sophisticated environmental systems, which integrate modern manufacturing practices. The ZEB certification and energy-efficient technologies underscore a commitment to sustainability, allowing visitors to experience firsthand how these innovative operations connect production with environmental stewardship. Moulds, forming machines, runners, and production tools reveal the precision behind objects that we often encounter only after they hit the shelves. This shift in perspective gives the museum broader cultural significance. A model, while small enough to fit in one hand, embodies decisions about proportion, movement, colour, tooling, and assembly. Each component must integrate into a larger whole, requiring the same clarity of thought found in many areas of industrial design.
The museum's setting amplifies this understanding, as production remains ongoing. It is organized around an operational factory, allowing visitors to witness the scale, machinery, and discipline of manufacturing as part of the exhibition. This presence lends credibility to a narrative that might otherwise remain concealed behind a finished product.

Creative concepts evolve into metal dies, precise tolerances, and repeatable parts before returning to the builder’s hands. ZEB certification, energy-saving and energy-generating equipment, automation, and labour-saving technologies are showcased as part of modern manufacturing practices. Visitors encounter sustainability through the facility's operations, linking production quality with resource awareness and future responsibility.
CIC, NOMURA Co., Ltd. has created a museum where scale constantly shifts. The architecture resembles a model kit; a tiny component reveals the logic of an industrial system; and thousands of completed works form a collective landscape. These changes in scale allow professional knowledge and popular enthusiasm to converge within the same experience.
The PLAMO DESIGN INDUSTRIAL INSTITUTE MUSEUM provides plastic modelling with a public cultural platform. It celebrates the ingenuity behind manufacturing, the joy of creation, and the community that continues to give each model a life beyond the production line.
