## Excerpt

Designed by KOKUYO Co., Ltd., Gateway Studio activates Takanawa Gateway City as a flexible public hub that utilizes modular, soil-inspired design to host community events, exhibitions, and localized information exchange.

## Editorial

At Takanawa Gateway City, KOKUYO has redefined the role of the information center, positioning it as a dynamic space rather than merely a venue for announcements and orientation. Gateway Studio, strategically situated directly in front of the north ticket gates at Takanawa Gateway Station, was inaugurated in March 2025, in conjunction with the establishment of the new mixed-use district. This facility serves as a publicly accessible venue for information dissemination, events, exhibitions, pop-up shops, and community engagement. Its purpose is intimately connected to the ongoing development of a district that is still evolving: a locale where interests can be exchanged, relationships can be formed, and the identity of the new neighbourhood can be cultivated in a communal environment.
KOKUYO frames the project with the guiding principle “Nurture your ideas.” This agricultural metaphor informs both the conceptual and material dimensions of the studio. Within a district designed around rail infrastructure, offices, and urban development, the interior design draws inspiration from visual representations of fields, rice paddies, soil, and seeds. Dyed cork was created through the analysis of the local soil's coloration, while vibrant green seed-shaped pins are employed to secure posters, booklets, and other printed materials. As such, information is literally “planted” within the surfaces, integrating the act of display into the project's identity.
The concept is further expressed architecturally through shelving that encircles the core of the studio. Thick, ribbon-like surfaces finished in cork provide a robust horizontal structure while enhancing the available display area: items can be exhibited on both the upper and lower surfaces. Behind these shelves, mirror-finished steel introduces a reflective quality and serves as a magnetic surface for supplementary materials. Consequently, this fixture transcends mere storage; it functions as an adaptable communication framework where material, display, and concept are intricately interwoven.
This adaptability is extended through a series of cube-shaped mobile units. The cork faces permit the use of the same seed-shaped pins, perforated boards accommodate conventional hooks, and shelf boards of varied dimensions can be interspersed between the cubes to augment the display surface. Mounted on castors, these units may be rearranged within the studio or relocated outdoors to the adjacent plaza on favourable days, thus enabling exhibitions and information to align with the vibrant public life surrounding the building.
This mobility is critical to the project’s engagement with the city. The studio occupies a transparent pavilion that is surrounded by greenery and outdoor seating, ensuring that its boundaries remain visually and functionally permeable. Displays may extend toward the plaza, visitors can transition seamlessly between the interior and exterior, and the space can adapt to differing requirements from one day to the next. The architecture provides a stable framework, whereas the furniture allows for programmatic flexibility.
 
KOKUYO extends this design language into the graphics utilized throughout the studio. Imagery of seeds being sown into dark soil is prevalent across posters, signage, and products, thereby reinforcing the interior concept in the studio’s visual identity. Given that the facility must accommodate frequently changing information, this carefully curated vocabulary of soil, seeds, and uncomplicated geometry ensures that new content remains legible without resulting in visual disarray.
The Gateway Studio lies in how its design provides a tangible language for the evolving nature of public life. The cork surfaces capture the changing displays, the mobile fixtures traverse the room and plaza, and the graphic system maintains coherence between diverse activities. KOKUYO does not represent Takanawa Gateway City as a concluded urban product; rather, the studio is conceptualized around the idea that a new district accrues meaning progressively through the information, conversations, and small acts of participation that individuals contribute over time.
