## Excerpt

KTX archiLAB transforms education in Osaka with Pure Semicircles, a high-performance cram school designed with custom window study counters, integrated biophilic privacy, and dynamic mirrored geometry to support digital-first learners.

## Editorial

For generations, juku, or cram schools, have played a significant role in the educational journey of Japanese students, serving as an intermediary between formal education and private study. However, the dynamics of student engagement within these institutions are undergoing transformation. At Pure Semicircles in Osaka, KTX archiLAB addresses a learning model increasingly reliant on laptops and tablets, which departs from the traditional arrangements of instructors, blackboards, and linear rows of desks. The spatial design reflects this paradigm shift, focusing on creating an environment conducive to concentration in an era characterized by individual, self-directed, screen-based learning.
 
Located on the fourth floor of a mixed-use building, the school effectively utilizes its perimeter. Extended counters positioned near the windows offer students individual workspaces bathed in natural light, while a larger double-sided table occupies the central area of the room. A planting feature traverses the space, providing a degree of separation among students without inducing isolation. The resulting layout creates a study environment in which privacy is achieved through strategic distance, orientation, and greenery rather than through conventional enclosed booths.
 
The project emphasizes the critical balance between concentration and comfort. Bright white walls and ceilings enhance the interior’s luminosity, yet KTX mitigates the visual intensity through the incorporation of timber blinds and substantial greenery. The vegetation is not merely ornamental; rather, it is integral to the organization of the learning space, softening sightlines between students and imbuing the environment associated with examination pressures with a more tranquil and domestic quality.
 
The title "Pure Semicircles" is particularly evident in the reception area. A semicircular counter interacts with a mirrored median wall, with its reflection completing the form as a full circle. Above this arrangement, rippled stainless steel transforms the ceiling into a reflective plane bordered by indirect linear lighting. This design creates a spatially ambiguous experience as solid and reflected forms overlap, extending the compact entrance beyond its physical dimensions.
 
KTX further employs the geometry of semicircles thoughtfully, avoiding a repetitive motif. Curved openings facilitate smoother transitions between study zones, while the central table features a rounded end to ease movement within the irregular floor plan. The semicircle serves various functions: it manipulates perception at the reception, removes hard corners in circulation areas, and ensures that a large shared surface integrates harmoniously among individual workstations within the study area.
 
Additionally, a consultation space situated between the reception and the main classroom acknowledges an often overlooked aspect of the cram school experience. Within this area, students and parents can meet with pedagogists in a designated room characterized by a quarter-circle ceiling that conceals indirect lighting and draws attention to a planted wall. This design creates a quieter setting for discussion while maintaining close proximity to the overall learning environment.
 
What renders Pure Semicircles compelling is not solely its integration of curves, mirrors, or greenery, but rather its responsiveness to an evolving culture of study. As instructional methods continue to evolve away from a singular focus at the front of the classroom, the design effectively embodies this transformation.
