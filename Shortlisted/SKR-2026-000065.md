## Excerpt

For The Prince Park Tower Tokyo’s renewal, Mitsui Designtec Co., Ltd. introduces "THE TOKYO FUSION," a design concept that dissolves the boundary between hospitality and the horizon. International orange and park green carry the spirit of Tokyo Tower and Shiba Park into the renewed upper-floor suites and social spaces, grounding the guest experience in the city’s most iconic panoramic landscape.

## Editorial

The Prince Park Tower Tokyo occupies a distinctive position in the capital. From its upper floors, Tokyo Tower rises in close proximity above the mature greenery of Shiba Park, bringing together two enduring aspects of the city: its metropolitan identity and its quieter landscape of gardens, temples and public spaces.
For the hotel’s twentieth anniversary, Mitsui Designtec renewed the 32nd and 33rd floors around this relationship. The concept, “THE TOKYO FUSION,” carries the colour, light and cultural character of the surroundings into the restaurants, bar, Club Lounge and five suites. The result is closely tied to its address, giving international visitors an immediate connection with Tokyo while offering local guests a fresh perspective on a familiar landmark.
Tokyo Tower has shaped the visual culture of the city since 1958. At this proximity, its international orange becomes part of the interior atmosphere, reflected through rust, amber and warm accent tones. Shiba Park contributes greens, earth colours and a gentler spatial register. Their presence changes throughout the day as sunlight, weather and evening illumination alter the relationship between the rooms and the view. This sensitivity to time is central to the hospitality experience. Breakfast, afternoon work, dinner and late-night drinks each carry a different pace. Mitsui Designtec has organized the upper floors around these changing patterns, allowing the interiors to move naturally between social activity, private conversation and moments of rest.
In the principal restaurant, the central kitchen provides an interior focal point equal to the Tokyo skyline. Set beneath a dark suspended canopy, it concentrates the movement, heat and precision of food preparation at the centre of the room. At the same time, perimeter seating maintains a direct relationship with Tokyo Tower. Dark timber, stone and brass give the restaurant visual depth after sunset, when the illuminated city becomes the dominant field beyond the glass. Dining is shaped by two simultaneous performances: the making of the food and the changing light of Tokyo. The bar establishes a closer relationship with the city after dark. Bottles and glassware form a luminous line beneath the windows, accompanied by amber seating and deeper finishes. The skyline extends above the counter, contributing to the room’s colour and atmosphere. The setting captures a familiar Tokyo ritual: ending the day with a drink while watching the city continue beyond the glass.
During daylight hours, the dining rooms respond more closely to Shiba Park. Green upholstery, botanical patterns and pale stone surfaces carry the landscape into the interior through colour and texture. Slender furniture and lighting preserve the openness of the glazing, giving trees, sky and shifting weather a clear presence within the rooms. This connection introduces a welcome sense of calm at the centre of Tokyo. The expanded Club Lounge reflects the changing role of the contemporary city hotel. Guests arrive for breakfast, continue working, meet colleagues, hold private discussions, or stay longer between appointments. Window-side desks, lounge seating and enclosed meeting rooms support these routines within a coherent setting. The lounge serves as a temporary workplace, drawing room, and social space for travellers whose days span business, culture, and leisure.
The journey towards the guest rooms continues the hotel’s relationship with movement and view. Patterned flooring guides the upper-level corridors and leads to five renewed suites. Each suite has a distinct accent palette, while the principal living and sleeping spaces are oriented towards Tokyo Tower, Shiba Park and the wider city. Within the suites, living, dining, working and sleeping areas form a connected domestic landscape. Low furniture maintains open sightlines to the windows, while glass and stone extend the rooms' visual depth. Artwork, textiles and colour introduce contemporary cultural references at a scale suited to daily life. Orange and rust tones echo the warmth of Tokyo Tower in one suite, while greys, blues and greens respond to the sky and park in another.
The variation gives returning guests a different experience on each visit while maintaining a consistent sense of place. Every suite belongs to the same architectural story, yet each presents its own interpretation of life above Tokyo.
The renewal of The Prince Park Tower Tokyo positions hospitality as a way of experiencing the city. Food, work, conversation, rest, design, and art are brought together in a setting shaped by one of Tokyo’s most recognizable landmarks and one of its established green spaces. Mitsui Designtec has developed the upper floors as a contemporary portrait of the capital, guided by colour, programme and changing views.
Tokyo is encountered through the glow of the tower, the greenery of the park, the activity of the kitchen and the changing atmosphere of the rooms. The hotel becomes a place from which to observe the city and participate in its daily rhythm, offering guests a relationship with Tokyo that continues beyond the view itself.
