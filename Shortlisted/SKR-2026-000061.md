## Excerpt

KTX archiLAB transforms traditional omakase design at The SAKAI Tokyo. The firm replaces conventional hinoki wood counters with custom rippled glass, creating a dynamic, light-reactive surface that enhances the dining experience.

## Editorial

At THE SAKAI Tokyo, KTX archiLAB transforms a significant aspect of Japanese dining by changing its material language. The traditional hinoki counter is replaced with custom rippled glass, which adds a fresh visual dimension to the omakase experience while still preserving the intimate connection between chef and guest, a hallmark of this dining format.
 
The choice of material is deliberate, as the counter is not merely a neutral piece of furniture in sushi culture; it is the focal point where preparation, presentation, and conversation converge. Each course traverses the same narrow distance, and every gesture is observed up close. By substituting timber with glass, the restaurant directs attention to light, reflection, and the ephemeral nature of each dish.
 
The design of the glass resembles flowing water, with its rippled surface capturing ambient light and creating dynamic highlights and shadows beneath the food. As each piece of sushi arrives, it briefly alters the visual composition before disappearing again, allowing the counter to continuously record these subtle changes throughout the meal.


This characteristic imparts a strong sense of time to the dining experience. Omakase emphasizes sequence and impermanence, as each course is prepared for a specific moment. The unique surface makes this transience visible, with colors, textures, and reflections shifting from one dish to the next, allowing the countertop to participate subtly in the progression of the meal.


Fabrication is equally significant, as the material must endure repeated service and cleaning while remaining comfortable for both chef and guest. It also needs to provide stability for plates and individual pieces. The undulation of the surface is meticulously controlled to maintain the practical requirements of a sushi counter while preserving the depth and irregularity that give it a distinctive character.
 
KTX archiLAB keeps the surrounding interior understated so that the material change remains prominent. Pale timber envelops the walls and ceiling, while dark chairs and black tracks provide contrast. The lighting is focused where food and hands need to be clearly seen, ensuring the room supports the counter without making the entire interior a visual spectacle. The elongated U-shaped layout ensures that every guest is close to both the chef and the preparation area. The luminous glass strip along the inner edge of the timber counter places each course in the brightest part of the design. Curved timber surfaces throughout the room soften the narrow proportions, creating a more intimate atmosphere for dining.
 
Arrival at the restaurant follows a quieter rhythm. A noren, along with a dark threshold and narrow illuminated passage, separates the restaurant from the shared building interior, allowing the main dining area to gradually reveal itself. A private dining room offers a more enclosed setting, while the principal counter maintains the directness and focus integral to the omakase experience.
 
THE SAKAI Tokyo brings innovation to the dining experience, focusing on precision and repetition. KTX archiLAB uses glass to enhance the presentation of each dish, creating a unique identity for the restaurant that is defined by light, movement, and the ephemeral moments when food is served, observed, and savoured.
