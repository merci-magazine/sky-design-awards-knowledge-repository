## Excerpt

Informal Design revitalizes a Hangzhou factory into a high-performance pet-human social hub, utilizing integrated circulation and adaptable materiality to bridge cross-species comfort.

## Editorial

The presence of companion animals is reshaping how cities approach shared spaces. Cafés, hotels, and social clubs now recognize pets as active participants. Healing Pets, a project by Informal Design in Hangzhou’s Xixiangli Cultural & Creative Park, embodies this shift architecturally.
The project preserves the factory’s timber roof and industrial frame, using the original volume for a new community space. Dog owners and their pets share the building, but the design recognizes their needs are distinct. Comfort and safety for both are considered from the outset.
This redefines “pet-friendly” design. At Healing Pets, friendliness is conveyed through behaviour. Dogs have space for movement and security, while owners benefit from clear circulation and comfortable seating. The interior is organized around this relationship.
A broad ramp traverses the centre, serving as a principal social space and connecting levels. Its playful slope makes circulation part of the experience. Blue and yellow accents mark thresholds and activity zones, supporting orientation for both pets and people.
Durable materials such as washed stone and marine plywood accommodate daily wear while retaining the building’s industrial scale. Furniture serves people and animals at different scales, with movable pieces allowing flexible use of space. Privacy is adjustable, with glass partitions offering openness or seclusion as needed for different activities.
The café is informal, with an open layout that allows animals to move freely while owners sit nearby. The industrial shell and timber elements provide continuity without a commercial feel.
Informal Design deliberately maintains a light architectural touch within the existing structure. Plywood screens and inserted volumes are visually distinct from the roof and frame, preserving the scale and memory of the former factory while allowing the new programme to assert its own identity.
Healing Pets reflects a broader trend of companion animals becoming part of urban social life, offering a model for shared spaces across species.
