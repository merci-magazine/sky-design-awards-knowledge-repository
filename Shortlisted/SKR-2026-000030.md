## Excerpt

Experience the dark, meditative world of Lei—a fragrance shop where SOL style skillfully combines urushi lacquer, Japanese hinoki, and candlelight to transform luxury retail through sensory immersion.

## Editorial

Fragrance is typically experienced through scent, but how it is discovered also depends on factors such as pace, attention, and atmosphere. At Lei, the SOL style transforms the act of selecting a fragrance into a more intimate sensory experience. Inspired by Jun’ichirō Tanizaki’s *In Praise of Shadows*, the space explores how darkness can enhance awareness of materials, reflections, and scents.
The experience begins outside on the street, where a dark film covers the glass façade, with only Lei’s circular “0” serving as an entry point. Passers-by can catch a glimpse of a narrow band of light and the flicker of candle flames within. This partial view creates an initial impression of the interior while maintaining an air of anticipation. Upon crossing the threshold, perceptions begin to shift. The ambient light diminishes, making the candles and illuminated mirrors the primary sources of light. As visitors' eyes adjust to the room, small variations in surfaces, movements, and reflections become more noticeable.
This carefully crafted atmosphere allows fragrances to unfold gradually. Scent is experienced through focused attention and proximity, supported by an interior that encourages visitors to appreciate subtle differences. The shop serves as a space for discovery, where light, wood, and lacquer contribute to the overall character of the experience.
Japanese cypress and ginkgo wood form the display surfaces. Hinoki is chosen for the central counter, evoking the fine fragrance and cultural significance of timber long used in sacred architecture. Ginkgo, known for its smooth grain, provides a more delicate setting for the fragrance products.
Both types of wood are hand-finished with traditional urushi lacquer. Candlelight and mirror reflections dance across the deep gloss, gradually revealing the wood grain as visitors shift their positions. The surfaces appear different from various angles, offering the interior a subtle sense of movement. The black bases of the fixtures blend into the shadows, allowing the lacquered tops, fragrance bottles, and reflected flames to appear visually suspended in the room. Raw concrete walls absorb light, introducing a mineral texture alongside the polished wood.
This interplay between gloss and roughness adds material depth to the shop. Hinoki, ginkgo, urushi, and concrete are experienced through their responses to limited light, allowing traditional craftsmanship to take on a contemporary role within the fragrance environment. The circular opening in the façade encapsulates the project's broader concept. Lei reveals its interior gradually, inviting visitors to follow a path from one illuminated fragment to a fuller sensory experience. Curiosity begins outside and continues through the evolving reflections within the room.
The SOL style has created a shop where shadows play an active role in hospitality. It slows down the experience, enhancing attention to scent and touch, and gives the selection of fragrance a personal sense of ceremony. At Lei, luxury is felt through the time and concentration devoted to a single, carefully observed moment.
