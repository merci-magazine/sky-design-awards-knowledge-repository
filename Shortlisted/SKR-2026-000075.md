## Excerpt

A-Residence, designed by A.N.D. and NOMURA, redefines luxury living in Tokyo, merging dramatic book-matched marble, bespoke timber joinery, and museum-quality art against the backdrop of the iconic Tokyo Tower skyline.

## Editorial

A-Residence exemplifies how various elements can be curated to create a tranquil environment. Located at a considerable height above Tokyo, with a view of Tokyo Tower at night, this residence showcases many features of contemporary luxury, including high-quality stone, custom joinery, bespoke furniture, curated art collections, and an impressive urban panorama. A.N.D., NOMURA Co., Ltd., applies a high degree of discipline to these elements. The project initiated with the living area and expanded into a comprehensive renovation, guided by ongoing collaboration with a client whose lifestyle spans multiple locations. The result is a thoughtfully constructed atmosphere in which materials, art, and views are in balance.
A notable aspect of this design is found at the entrance. A pine-themed artwork is positioned at the center of a dimly lit gallery, suspended above hand-carved stone vessels with polished surfaces reminiscent of still water. A circular opening of light highlights the composition, while the skyline of Tokyo glimmers beyond the window. This scene combines elements of installation art with the traditional function of an entrance, modifying the visitor’s pace. In just a few steps, the city remains visible, yet its noise is replaced with shadows, textures, stone, and the silhouette of a tree.
This sense of control extends throughout the residence. The open living and dining area possesses significant scale, yet it does not feel overly grand. Low-profile furniture maintains a grounded center of gravity, while timber walls add warmth to the space. A large stone dining table provides enough visual weight to stand confidently next to the skyline, and a suspended light fixture moves gracefully within the room. Though Tokyo Tower can be seen through the glazing, the interior does not solely frame the view; the architecture establishes its own visual rhythm, integrating the city into the overall composition.
Stone plays a central role in this design. A.N.D. carefully selected marble from raw blocks, developing its placement across floors and columns through extensive book-matching techniques. This decision is important, as the material is treated with the scale of architecture, rather than being applied as isolated decorative features. The veining flows across large surfaces, wraps around structural elements, and changes character as light interacts with the space. The designers refer to stone as forming a landscape, a term that is fitting in this context. Its natural patterns create a secondary topography within the residence that engages in a quiet dialogue with the metropolitan landscape outside.
Timber and washi paper introduce a softer tonal quality amidst the material intensity. Doors and storage solutions are integrated into expansive planes of joinery, allowing the wood grain to remain continuous while the architecture recedes when necessary. Smaller objects are displayed in illuminated niches, while larger artworks occupy generous wall spaces; furniture is positioned with the assurance of pieces intended for long-term placement. Art retains its significance without becoming an imposition on daily life. The residence conveys the notion that these objects belong to an individual, and that the spaces will continue to acquire meaning through use.
This perspective enhances the appeal of A-Residence. Luxury is often expressed through the accumulation of materials and objects.  Moreover, the project addresses the concept of ownership over time with thoughtful consideration. The client's existing furniture and cherished artworks were preserved and relocated alongside newly commissioned pieces. Marble was procured in block form and utilized consistently throughout the residence, allowing for natural variation to be incorporated into the design while minimizing waste. These choices contribute to a sense of sustainability grounded in longevity, continuity, and attachment: materials are selected for durability, and possessions with established histories are incorporated as integral components of the home.
