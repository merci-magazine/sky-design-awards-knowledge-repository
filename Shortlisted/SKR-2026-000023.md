## Excerpt

Each design redefines the Hakusan Megane Shanghai flagship store. By translating eyewear hinge mechanics into custom iron and reclaimed elm joinery, the retail interior connects Japanese craftsmanship with the historic Zhang Yuan district.

## Editorial

A pair of glasses is one of the most personal items a person can wear. It sits close to the face, alters the way the world is perceived, and gradually becomes part of an individual's identity. For Hakusan Megane, a company with a history dating back to 1883, opening its first store in mainland China meant creating a space where that intimacy could merge with a new city.
The boutique in Shanghai is situated in Zhang Yuan, a late-nineteenth-century garden complex that has been restored into a cultural and commercial quarter. Each Design approached the project with a focus on continuity: they aimed to preserve the atmosphere and display style associated with Hakusan Megane’s Japanese stores while allowing Shanghai's materials and history to influence the new location. The retail design draws inspiration from the construction of eyewear itself. Hinges and temples are small, almost invisible components, yet they determine how a frame opens, closes, and adapts to the wearer. Each Design expanded this concept into a system of joints for shelves, tables, and cabinets, giving the interior a character that directly relates to the product. This approach creates a subtle brand identity: Hakusan Megane is recognized by how its elements connect, rotate, and support one another. The store doesn’t rely on oversized graphics to convey its identity; instead, the precision of an eyeglass frame is echoed in the furniture and display system.
The layout follows the display logic of Hakusan Megane’s Shibuya store, maintaining a familiar order across the brand. In Shanghai, this system is reinterpreted using materials selected for their age, texture, and connection to Zhang Yuan. The outcome connects the two locations through construction methods rather than visual replication. Reclaimed elm is used for the tables and cabinets; salvaged from older timber buildings, this wood retains variations in grain, colour, and surface, giving each piece a sense of history. Hundreds of smaller sections are joined using mortise-and-tenon finger joints, allowing the craftsmanship of construction to remain visible.
The same joining principle applies to solid iron. Eight-millimetre panels are crafted into wall shelving with thoughtfully integrated connections, lending the heavy material a lighter appearance. Timber and metal share a common language of precision, while their weathered surfaces place them within a shared material history. Water-corrugated glass, also used in other Hakusan Megane stores, introduces a delicate optical element. Light dances across its rippled surface, casting shifting shadows over the frames and materials behind them. The glass fosters a direct relationship with vision, allowing clarity and distortion to enhance the display experience.
For visitors, the atmosphere unfolds through careful observation. The grain of reclaimed elm, the oxidation of iron, the edges of joints, and the silhouettes of frames all offer rewards at a smaller scale. The store reflects the process of choosing eyewear: through proportion, comfort, detail, and the gradual recognition of what feels right. The protected building provides a setting imbued with memory. New fixtures are integrated within an inherited shell, allowing the boutique to connect with Zhang Yuan without simply duplicating its past. Japanese craftsmanship and Shanghai history converge through material, touch, and construction.
Hakusan Megane Shanghai represents international expansion as a thoughtful act of translation. Each Design has carried the brand’s values into a new city through the mechanics of eyewear, a disciplined display, and materials shaped by time. The store feels distinctly like Hakusan Megane while remaining inseparable from the local context in which it now resides.
