## Editorial

“This project is not just an office; it is a space that thinks.” This simple idea underpins the SPARX Guest Area & Lounge, designed by Tomoyuki Kobayashi of KOKUYO for the investment firm's headquarters. The design reflects the corporate philosophy that “the macro emerges from the accumulation of the micro.” The interior space serves as an active medium through which this concept can be perceived, heard, and physically experienced. The company introduces its identity through the visitor’s journey, embedding that identity in the experience.
The journey begins in a long reception area with white tones that overlooks Tokyo. Indirect lighting traces the edges of the ceiling, while pale surfaces diffuse daylight. A continuous timber bench follows the perimeter, gently curving around an indoor tree, creating a calm and unhurried atmosphere. Ornamentation is minimal, allowing light, proportions, and material textures to define the environment. A large digital artwork occupies one wall, presenting financial information in the soft form of a landscape rather than through traditional charts and trading screens.
This artwork is sourced from Japan’s EDINET system, a public database of corporate financial disclosures. Historical data is processed through a generative system inspired by the behaviour of slime mould, revealing connections between companies that might otherwise remain hidden. In this installation, numbers transform into colour, movement, and spatial relationships, making finance resemble a living ecology, where individual entities respond to one another within a continuously evolving system. This approach makes a complex subject more approachable without diminishing its sophistication, giving emotional weight to information typically understood through analysis alone.
A bronze-toned sliding threshold marks the transition from the reception area to a dark corridor. Its softly reflective surface captures a blurred image of the bright room before opening into shadow. The contrast is immediate: brightness gives way to darkness, stillness acquires rhythm, and the expansive reception narrows into a focused passage. This sequence operates with cinematic precision, using contrast and duration to prepare the visitor for a different mode of attention.
As visitors move along the corridor, real-time movements from the Nikkei 225 stock index are translated into synchronized visual and auditory patterns. Streams of light flow across a triptych of screens as individual stock fluctuations gather, separate, and form new alignments. Sound adds temporal depth to these movements, allowing the data to be perceived as a collective rhythm. This installation expresses emergence through the direct experience of micro-level events accumulating and interacting until a larger order becomes recognizable. SPARX’s investment philosophy is interwoven with the architecture, manifested through movement, sound, and varying intensities.
Digital art by siro, Silica, and backspacetokyo, combined with sound design by Oyster and lighting from ModuleX, function as parts of a cohesive spatial composition. The screens align with the path of travel; the darkness minimizes visual distractions, while the pale floor helps maintain orientation. A timber-lined opening interrupts the black corridor with a sudden burst of warmth, using material contrast to signal a change in program and pace. This interplay of white and black, silence and movement, smooth and textured surfaces creates a tactile language through which the company’s thinking is gradually revealed.
The journey continues into a gallery-like space designed as an evolving platform for SPARX’s social vision and future initiatives. This adaptable framework can accommodate changing content and new forms of presentation as the company evolves. Ideas are allowed to emerge through continuity, adaptation, and repeated encounters. The architecture avoids constraining the organization within a single corporate message; instead, it provides a setting in which its priorities can be revealed over time.


Adjacent lounges and meeting rooms shift the experience to a more intimate scale. Pale timber wraps the walls, ceilings, and integrated furnishings, while upholstered benches, shared tables, and acoustic surfaces facilitate waiting, conversation, reflection, and collaboration. The meeting room combines a formal conference table with a continuous bench and smaller tables along its edge, allowing for different patterns of use to coexist. In the communal lounge, a broad table faces a recessed refreshment counter, creating a social environment conducive to everyday activities such as making a drink, reading, exchanging ideas, or pausing between meetings. Controlled lighting and views of the city offer a calming atmosphere following the intensity of the data corridor.
These quieter rooms complete the project’s conceptual cycle. Small thoughts, informal exchanges, and individual observations are nurtured, allowing them to evolve into meaningful collective actions. Trust, focus, and openness are treated as vital components of the overall experience.
