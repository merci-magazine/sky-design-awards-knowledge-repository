## Editorial

At Ono Station in Okuma, CREVA OKUMA and KUMA SUN TERRACE occupy ground that was once the town centre, then remained inaccessible after the nuclear accident until restrictions were lifted in 2022. FIELD FOUR DESIGN OFFICE approaches the site as more than a new municipal facility: it is an attempt to restore the ordinary settings in which a community meets, works and remembers.


The architecture is organised around a central plaza open to both station and town square. Its split volumes are rotated against the surrounding roads, a measured adjustment that resolves changes in level and creates barrier-free routes through the site. A long, single-pitch roof recalls the profile of the Abukuma Mountains, while artificial-turf mounds, water, native planting and movable furniture give the plaza enough informality for lunch breaks, festivals or an unplanned conversation.


CREVA OKUMA builds a civic programme vertically around this shared ground. The first-floor hall can open for events; an atrium lounge is anchored by tapestry art; coworking areas, an open pantry and rental offices occupy the second level; and a rooftop garden extends public life above. Photographs and objects from before the disaster are placed within these contemporary rooms, allowing memory to remain present without turning the building into a memorial.


The material choices reinforce that balance. Chestnut, ash and larch sourced locally support regional forestry and reduce environmental impact, while the wider development forms part of Okuma's ambition for a zero-carbon town powered by local renewable energy. Here, reconstruction is expressed through access, shade, workspaces and places to sit—the modest but essential apparatus of daily life.
