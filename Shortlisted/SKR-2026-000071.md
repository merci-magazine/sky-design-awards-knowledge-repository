## Excerpt

## Editorial

BASE LAYER HOTEL NAGOYA NISHIKI reimagines the traditional business hotel format by giving it a broader cultural role. MITSUI Designtec's renovation of this 22-year-old property aims to create a space that caters to work, rest, media consumption, wellness, and a deeper connection to the city—all encapsulated under the concept of a “Culture Business Hotel.” The name "Base Layer" hints at its purpose. A base layer is the first item worn, close to the body, designed to support everything that follows. Similarly, the hotel prioritizes essentials like sleep, bathing, storage, and work, then enhances these elements through colour, art, technology, and sauna culture.
 
This approach is ideal for short urban stays. A guest may arrive for business, spend an evening streaming a film, use the laundry facilities, book a sauna, socialize with friends nearby, and leave the next morning without any of these activities competing for attention. Each has enough space to feel intentional.
 
The renovation focuses on the areas that guests frequent the most. The entrance is accentuated with a dark stone base, an illuminated canopy, and a deep green threshold, while the reception area adopts this palette in a subtler way. Timber ceilings, dark counters, and long built-in seating establish a sense of order without overcrowding the space. Green is a recurring theme throughout the project. It can be found in upholstery, corridor walls, shower enclosures, signage, and selected joinery, giving the hotel a recognizable identity across various spaces. This controlled use of colour also serves as a navigation aid within the retained building.
 
Guest rooms are compact but thoughtfully planned. Beds, storage, work surfaces, and bathrooms are arranged with minimal wasted space, while pale timber, warm grey, and black metal maintain a restrained visual language. Larger Japanese-style rooms offer low seating, kitchenettes, and more flexible floor space, accommodating families, groups, and longer stays. One notable feature is the green shower booth. Its translucent enclosure provides visibility within the room, transforming a functional aspect into a part of the brand's identity. Privacy is available when needed, while the glass maintains an open and airy feel.
 
The rooms also reflect how guests spend their downtime today. Large screens, shelves, pegboards, and graphic panels support streaming, gaming, and the use of personal devices, ensuring the room feels modern without conforming to a specific lifestyle image. Wellness also plays a significant role in the hotel's identity. Selected suites include private löyly saunas with cold bathing, integrated into the overall experience. Bookable sauna rooms extend this wellness ritual throughout the building, with heat, cold, and recovery processes organized around "totonou," the Japanese practice of physical and mental reset.
 
This program adds value beyond simple accommodation. The sauna can become a compelling reason to stay, while comfortably coexisting alongside the practical aspects of work, laundry, and media use.
 
The retained building also contributes to the project's character. Existing hardware and infrastructure are preserved where feasible, with investments focused on surfaces, equipment, and functions that enhance daily use. This approach results in a focused renovation that respects the building’s original structure while providing a new identity through selective changes.
 
BASE LAYER HOTEL NAGOYA NISHIKI combines the efficiency of a business hotel with a broader vision for urban stays. MITSUI Designtec has integrated work, rest, local culture, digital life, and sauna traditions within one cohesive framework, giving the hotel a contemporary relevance that goes beyond just an overnight stay.
