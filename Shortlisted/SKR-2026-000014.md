## Editorial Rewrite

Hakodate has long welcomed travellers, blending trains, ferries, and market streets that lead to its harbour, gradually rising toward Mount Hakodate. One standout option for visitors is OMO5 Hakodate, conveniently located across from the train station. The hotel serves as a gateway to the city, allowing guests to immerse themselves in its atmosphere before they even unpack.

Renovated by fan Inc. for Hoshino Resorts, OMO5 is inspired by the concept of "120% Hakodate." This approach transcends simply showcasing local landmarks, focusing instead on how guests can connect with the city, where they gather, what they notice, and how the hotel encourages exploration beyond its doors.

Upon arrival, guests are greeted by a large stained-glass piece at the reception. This artwork, featuring the star-shaped Goryokaku and scenic depictions of the harbour and surrounding landscape, establishes a strong sense of place. Below, the reception desk is designed with calm precision.

The hotel’s public spaces adopt a more casual, urban vibe. OMO Base reflects the character of Hakodate’s port and warehouse architecture, providing comfortable furniture designed for conversation and relaxation. Its greatest asset is the neighbourhood knowledge it offers. Available maps and local recommendations make the lobby a living guide to the city, inviting guests to discover markets, independent shops, and everyday local life. This connection gives OMO5 Hakodate its unique charm; it’s not just about showcasing the destination but about inviting travellers to engage with it, whether wandering through the market in the morning or exploring the harbour in the afternoon.

Dining adds another layer to the Hakodate experience. Inspired by the geometry of Goryokaku, the restaurant’s live kitchen features maritime elements like fishing nets and squid, tying it closely to the city’s culture. These details introduce humour and familiarity while enhancing the dining experience.

The dining atmosphere varies, transitioning from the open feel of the new dining hall to the cozier ambiance of existing rooms, mirroring the hotel’s daily rhythm. Whether it's a quick breakfast, a lively dinner with friends, or a serene return after a day of exploration, each meal captures the essence of Hakodate’s spirit.

In the guest rooms, the connection to Hakodate deepens. Personal touches, such as illustrations of Mount Hakodate’s stunning night view and subtle squid motifs, reward attentive guests while maintaining a peaceful atmosphere. For design professionals, this project offers insight into how regional identity can inform hospitality design without simply serving decorative purposes. Each design element is functional: stained glass signals arrival, maps guide exploration, the live kitchen shares local lore while fostering social interaction, and room details extend the city's character into personal spaces.

For travellers, the experience feels intuitive. OMO5 Hakodate encapsulates the essence of the city, making it approachable, relatable, and filled with potential. Fan Inc. has crafted a hotel that integrates seamlessly with Hakodate, inviting guests to explore its stories and encouraging them to return to the streets with a deeper understanding of their surroundings.

## Editor’s Review

The project is the connection between local narrative and practical hospitality. The stained glass, neighbourhood map, dining references and room graphics each support a different stage of the guest journey.

OMO Base is especially effective as an urban living room. Varied furniture, open planning and direct neighbourhood information give the lobby a role beyond waiting, encouraging guests to discuss, plan and return with new knowledge of the city.

The balance between humour and spatial discipline is well judged. Squid lanterns, fishing-net lights and concealed motifs remain memorable because they are contained within clear circulation, contrasting room scales and a coherent material framework.

## Peggy’s Editor’s Note

A hotel can celebrate local identity and still leave its guests isolated from the place outside. OMO5 Hakodate avoids that contradiction by connecting interior references with practical exploration. The stained glass establishes arrival, the neighbourhood map guides visitors into the city, and the restaurant turns market and port culture into an active dining experience.

The project’s humour works because it is balanced by a clear hospitality programme and contrasting spatial moods. fan Inc. gives guests several ways to engage with Hakodate, from direct information to gradual visual discovery. Local character deepens curiosity about the city instead of replacing the experience of it.

The restaurant’s central cooking area features star geometry inspired by Goryokaku. Guests can circulate around the active kitchen, using the city landmark as a functional spatial organizer.

Lanterns and net-like pendant fixtures reference local fishing traditions above the tables. Their scale and warm light create a lively atmosphere without overwhelming the dining experience.

IMAGE 07 — CONTRASTING DINING HALLS

The brighter, extended hall and the lower, existing room provide distinct levels of openness. This contrast offers guests a choice between a lively communal dining experience and a more intimate atmosphere.

IMAGE 08 — GUEST-ROOM GRAPHIC DETAIL

Illustrated nighttime imagery and concealed squid motifs introduce Hakodate at a more subtle level. These references remain secondary to the essentials of sleep, storage, and comfort, revealing themselves through gradual discovery.

## Drawing and Spatial Analysis

No dimensions of interior design and architectural plans or sections were supplied in the accessible project folder. The spatial reading is based on the original project overview and submitted photography.

The public sequence moves from a compressed dark exterior canopy into a taller reception space centred on the stained-glass wall. OMO Base extends beyond as an open social field divided by brick piers, furniture clusters and changes in ceiling condition.

Reception, neighbourhood information, lounge seating and café service remain visually connected. Guests can identify each function from the main circulation route without the floor becoming one undifferentiated hall.

OMO Dining appears to combine existing and extended areas. The live kitchen forms an active centre, while seating is distributed between a bright open hall and lower, more enclosed zones. This arrangement supports different group sizes and levels of activity.

Guest-room circulation shifts towards lower light and reduced colour before entering the private rooms. The overall organisation grades experience from public information and social exchange towards quieter individual discovery.
