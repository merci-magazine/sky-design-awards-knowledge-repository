## Excerpt

Lost Villa. Huanglong Island Lighthouse Hotel steps lightly across a dramatic island site, preserving native reefs while framing seasonal sunrise and coastal life.

## Editorial

Reaching Huanglong Island is an integral part of the experience. The ferry journey from Zhoushan's main island takes about two and a half hours, creating a stretch of open water that sets visitors apart from their daily routines. At Dongjutou Cape, the island's easternmost point, the Lost Villa Huanglong Island Lighthouse Hotel offers an invitation to explore the terrain, weather, and coastal life of the Zhoushan archipelago.
Huanglong Island is also undergoing demographic changes. An aging population and declining local vitality have prompted new ideas about how tourism can benefit its future. The hotel is an early step in this broader development, aiming to create a destination rooted in the island's inherent qualities: its remote setting, native vegetation, exposed reefs, and strong relationship with the sea.
The hotel site slopes down by nearly thirty meters towards the coast, featuring three protected reef formations embedded in the terrain. WJ STUDIO has utilized this topography to design the hotel as a series of interlocking, stair-like volumes. Independent foundations allow the buildings to settle among the rocks, while rooms, roofs, and terraces follow the changing elevation of the ridge.
This design creates an experience of movement for guests. An outdoor walkway descends between Block A and Block B, incorporating the shifting slope, sea air, and surrounding vegetation into the journey between shared spaces and guest rooms. The island remains ever-present throughout the day, encountered not only through the views but also through each transition.
Block A centers around an open rock hall where the original reef is still visible beneath the building. The architecture extends above it like a canopy, providing shelter while allowing visitors to closely approach the weathered stone. This hall introduces the landscape's history through direct experience, making the reef's textures and scale integral to the hotel's arrival experience.
This interaction gives the project a distinctive sense of place. The rock bears the marks of wind, water, and time, connecting the contemporary hotel with a geological story that predates tourism on the island. Guests are encouraged to notice the ground beneath them before turning to the horizon.
Block B houses the accommodations within three separate volumes inspired by the dispersed arrangement of Huanglong Island's existing villages. This composition grants the hotel the character of a small settlement along the ridge, with each building having its own relationship to the landscape.
The windows are designed to respond to the seasonal movement of sunrise, framing different angles of light during winter and summer. Morning becomes part of the spatial experience, allowing the atmosphere of each room to shift with the time of year. The architecture offers guests a way to connect with the island through light, weather, and repeated observations.
The layout also emphasizes outdoor movement as a central aspect of the stay. Walking between buildings provides moments of exposure to wind and sea before reaching the next sheltered interior. These short journeys foster a strong connection with the landscape, enriching daily activities from breakfast to returning to a room.
Spanning 5,000 square meters, the hotel accommodates a substantial program, yet its clustered form aligns with the ridge's scale and pattern. The separate volumes create a varied silhouette across the site, allowing the reef, vegetation, and changes in elevation to remain clearly visible.
This relationship between hospitality and place gives the project significance beyond its architectural design. Visitors come to Huanglong Island to experience the unique character of travel, distance, and the coastal environment. The hotel provides comfort and facilities for an extended stay while introducing guests to the island's existing terrain and settlement culture.
Tourism also fosters a new connection between visitors and the local community. As Huanglong Island contemplates its future, the hotel establishes a contemporary destination that highlights the island's landscape and encourages ongoing engagement with it. The long-term narrative will develop through the people who work there, the guests who return, and how the destination integrates into local life.
Lost Villa Huanglong Island Lighthouse Hotel presents the island through an evolving connection with rock, slope, wind, and seasonal light. WJ STUDIO has created a place where the journey across the water continues after arrival, guiding guests deeper into the coastal landscape and fostering a more thoughtful experience of time spent on Huanglong Island.
