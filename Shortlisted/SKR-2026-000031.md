## Excerpt

YTD addresses the penthouse privacy paradox in this Tokyo residence by combining Japanese hikido sliding doors with switchable glass technology. This design allows for quick transitions between expansive 120-meter skyline views and fully enclosed, private quarters.

## Editorial

Living high above Tokyo transforms the concept of privacy. At 120 meters above the city, the skyline becomes an integral part of daily life, visible from morning until night. In A Residence, YTD has designed the penthouse to allow this extraordinary view to flow throughout the home while ensuring that each room maintains the intimacy necessary for everyday living.
 
The full renovation begins with a simple idea: the apartment can adapt to the time of day. During daylight hours, the living, dining, kitchen, bathroom, and bedroom areas form a continuous sequence. In the evening, the same interior transforms into a series of private rooms, each with its own unique atmosphere.
 
YTD achieves this transformation using hikido, the sliding doors traditionally associated with Japanese architecture. Their movement allows rooms to connect and separate easily, giving residents control over how much of the home remains open at any given moment. When the doors are drawn aside, the view extends from the living and dining areas toward the bathroom and bedroom. Daylight follows this same path, reaching deeper into the space and allowing the changing sky to become part of various daily rituals. Activities such as bathing, resting, and moving through the apartment all occur within a broader awareness of the city beyond the glass.
 
This openness gives the penthouse a harmonious rhythm during the day. The rooms feel interconnected through light and perspective, with the skyline visible across multiple spaces at once. Moving through the home reveals different alignments among the buildings, sky, and horizon, allowing the view to evolve depending on where the resident stands. Privacy is easily restored through the same architectural system. Closing the sliding doors re-establishes the independence of the bedroom, bathroom, and living areas. Instant-dimming glass adds another layer of control, shifting from transparency to opacity whenever more enclosure is needed.
 
The combination of sliding doors and switchable glass blends traditional spatial thinking with contemporary technology. Both elements respond seamlessly to use, accommodating changes in privacy without disrupting the interior's clarity. After sunset, the residence's character becomes more intimate. Indirect lighting replaces the expansive brightness of the day, highlighting shadows, depth, and the boundaries between rooms. Though the metropolitan view remains, the interior adopts a calmer, more enclosed atmosphere.
 
For the resident, this adaptability creates a home that supports various lifestyles. The apartment can open fully for socializing and then close for more private bathing, resting, or quiet concentration. This choice is available throughout the day, allowing the interior to respond naturally to changing routines.
 
A Residence demonstrates that the appeal of a panoramic home extends beyond the view itself. YTD has made openness adjustable, giving residents the freedom to enjoy the Tokyo skyline while deciding when to share it across the home and when to draw the rooms gently inward.
