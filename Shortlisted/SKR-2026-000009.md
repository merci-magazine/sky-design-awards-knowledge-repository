## Editorial Rewrite

HIKU: Transforming the Everyday Door Handle into Artware

A door handle is often overlooked as a mere utilitarian component of a building. However, HIKU, created by OFT Studio in collaboration with UNION Japan, seeks to elevate this commonplace item into an expressive and artistic experience. The designers refer to HIKU as “Artware,” a fusion of engineering, ergonomics, and sculpture that engages users on multiple levels.

At its core, each lever or pull begins with the fundamental shape of a rectangular block. This block features straight edges and controlled surfaces, ensuring a clear and functional relationship with the door. The form appears as if it has been torn apart from opposing directions, revealing a texture reminiscent of molten metal beneath its meticulously crafted exterior.

This distinct rupture is central to the collection's visual identity. The interplay of polished and textured surfaces creates an interruption at the point of contact, suggesting that physical force has been permanently integrated into the object. This stable structure captures a moment of deformation while preserving its essential functionality.

The name "HIKU," derived from the Japanese word meaning “to pull,” profoundly influences both the design and the user experience. The handle invites interaction, evoking the sensation of an object pulled from either side, leaving an energetic trace within the material. By starting with this action, HIKU consciously moves away from conventional decorative motifs. The ergonomic qualities of the handle are equally noteworthy. The changing contour of the torn area provides a natural grip for the palm and fingers, clearly differentiating the touch zone from the rigid outer surfaces. This design ensures that visual contrast and tactile orientation coalesce in one intuitive space.

In the lever model, this concept is condensed into a horizontal object. While retaining the recognizable features of standard hardware—a rectangular base and bar—the center morphs into a fluid depression where the thumb and palm naturally make contact before applying force to the mechanism. Meanwhile, the pull-handle models extend this gesture across a longer span, with variations featuring ruptures placed strategically along their lengths. Some designs present a twist between fixing points, affecting the reflection and grip, thereby making the relationship between opposing forces and material transformation explicit.

Surface finish plays a pivotal role in the design's impact. Controlled matte or polished planes meet sharply defined, irregular textures, creating striking contrasts. Light interacts dynamically with the deformed areas, enhancing depth and ensuring that the hardware remains visually distinct from every angle.  Inspiration for HIKU draws from contemporary sculpture, particularly the bronze works of artists like Romain Langlois. The design encapsulates the idea of a rigid exterior revealing a fluid interior, resulting in a handle that can withstand repeated physical contact—a sculptural reference that is continuously tested through use.

UNION Japan's manufacturing expertise provides practical credibility to the concept, ensuring that the hardware accommodates fixation, torque, surface durability, and frequent operation while maintaining precise tolerances. The apparently spontaneous nature of the torn texture is achieved through rigorous controlled production.

The synergy between concept and manufacturing marks a significant strength of the project. OFT Studio offers an expressive proposition, while UNION refines the design into functional hardware. Each aspect of the collaboration is essential: without the engineering precision, the form would remain an abstract concept; without the visual rupture, it would be just another generic component.

Ultimately, HIKU transforms the perception of door hardware. While typical hardware often goes unnoticed until after the larger door is acknowledged, HIKU commands a concentrated moment of attention at the threshold. Users are drawn to the object before engaging with it, thereby completing the design through the simple act of opening. The design’s scale is significant; it captures attention while remaining discreet enough to harmonize with its environment, avoiding competition with surrounding elements. When installed on a minimalist door, HIKU serves as a tactile focal point, eliminating the need for a larger decorative system.

In essence, HIKU illustrates how an everyday component can embody narrative and artistry without sacrificing practicality. The geometry interacts with the user’s palm, force translates into movement, and the object consistently registers the presence of the individual. Each opening of the door embodies and reinforces the original design concept, establishing HIKU as a powerful example of how functional design can elevate the commonplace into the extraordinary.

## Editor’s Review

HIKU combines visual appeal with practical functionality. The sculptural design element is thoughtfully placed where the hand connects, enhancing the user's physical experience. This blend of aesthetics and ergonomics ensures that the design serves a purpose beyond decoration. Consistency is a key feature of the collection; the relationship between the precise rectangular form and the fluid center is evident in all lever and pull-handle formats. This cohesive identity allows for various lengths and operational modes while maintaining a unified look. The collaboration between OFT Studio and UNION Japan is fundamental to this achievement. While the irregular texture suggests spontaneity, the final product results from careful fabrication, durable finishes, and the mechanical precision expected from quality architectural hardware.

The project folder did not include any technical drawings, dimensions, or installation details. This analysis is based on the original designer's submission and five product photographs.

The collection features a rectangular bar as its common geometric base. Lever models connect this bar to a compact square or rectangular rose, while pull handles extend it between two projecting fixing points.

The deformed zone is strategically placed where the hand is expected to apply force. On the lever, it occupies the central-to-inner section of the bar; on the pull handle, it interrupts the span between the fixings and may extend through a longer twisting sequence.

The straight outer surfaces maintain compatibility with contemporary doors and frames. The irregular center provides grip, reflection, and identity without changing the recognizable operating direction of the hardware.

The photographs suggest various finish combinations, including darker matte blocks and brighter polished accents. This contrast can enhance both visual recognition and tactile differentiation, although finish specifications and performance data were not included in the submitted materials.

## Original Designer Submission — Verbatim

HIKU (引く)

Overview

Designed in 2025, HIKU (引く) is a premium door lever and handle collection born from a production collaboration between OFT Studio and UNION Japan. Moving entirely away from standard utility, this project conceptualizes architectural hardware as "Artware" —a seamless intersection of functional product engineering and expressive Door Art.

Design Vision: From Object to Art

The design challenges traditional hardware conventions by treating the object as an expressive installation on a door:

The Artistic Bridge: It draws conceptual inspiration from contemporary sculpture, explicitly referencing the 2002 bronze works of Romain Langlois.

Spatial Fluidity: This connection translates the expressive qualities of Wall Art into high-touch Door Art, transforming a mundane daily interaction into an artistic experience.

Material Transformation & Texture Contrast

The core visual narrative of HIKU revolves around a dramatic material transformation that breaks a rigid geometric form into fluid life:

The Origin: The hardware begins its design lifecycle as a regular, clean rectangular block.

The Tearing: The form is dynamically created by simulating a physical tearing of the block from the opposite side.

Texture Contrast: Where the block tears open, an intentional finishes change occurs. This high-contrast zone reveals an organic texture that looks like molten metal melting inside the regular rectangular block.

Human Touch & Name Meaning

The name HIKU (引く) translates from Japanese to mean "pulling from both sides." This raw physical action is permanently frozen into the ergonomics of the hardware:

Form Follows Energy: The design captures the exact moment human energy alters a material, leaving a basic form of a door lever or door handle embedded with genuine human touches.

Ergonomic Intuition: By infusing these organic human touches directly into a basic silhouette, the finished door handle effortlessly contours to the palm, making the physical act of opening a door feel intensely interactive.

About the Collaborators

OFT Studio: An spatial design firm acting as the creative visionary behind this design concept.

UNION Japan: The manufacturing and production collaborator specializing in architectural hardware, including door handles and lever handles.
