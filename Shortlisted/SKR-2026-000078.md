## Excerpt

RENS. NOMURA Co., Ltd. revitalizes the RIHGA Royal Hotel Osaka by bridging the building’s historic architectural legacy with contemporary Japanese craft. Drawing on the concept of "The Fusion of Traditional Beauty and Water," the design integrates flowing metalwork, Nishijin weaving, and calligraphic motifs to connect the hotel to its Nakanoshima riverfront setting and the city's broader social memory.

## Editorial

Hotels often become a part of a city’s memory long before they are recognized as significant architecture. It becomes part of a city’s social landscape, shaped by generations of arrivals, celebrations, meetings and returns. RIHGA Royal Hotel Osaka belongs to this category. Its identity has developed alongside modern Osaka, giving the building a cultural presence that extends beyond the conventions of luxury hospitality.
In April 2025, the hotel began a new chapter as RIHGA Royal Hotel Osaka, Vignette Collection. The renewal by RENS. NOMURA Co., Ltd. connects IHG Hotels & Resorts’ international platform with the architectural legacy of Isoya Yoshida, whose work brought Japanese aesthetics and Western influences into a distinctive and enduring composition.
The hotel’s location on Nakanoshima provides the project with its strongest reference point. Set between the Dojima and Tosabori Rivers, the island developed through Osaka’s commercial and cultural exchange. Water carried goods and ideas through the city, while Nakanoshima grew into a district of civic institutions, art, business and public life. The hotel’s description as “Floating Nakanoshima” captures this relationship between architecture and geography.
“The Fusion of Traditional Beauty and Water” converts that setting into a contemporary hospitality experience. The river appears through reflection, texture, movement and light, expressed across metalwork, Nishijin weaving, projected imagery, flooring and calligraphy. The theme remains consistent while each craft and material contributes its own character.
At the entrance, slender metal pipes form a new architectural gate. Their varied lengths and positions create a changing surface that responds to daylight, illumination and the movement of guests. Finished by specialist craftspeople, the installation combines the precision of metalwork with the fluidity of water, giving the hotel a renewed presence within the Nakanoshima streetscape.
The experience of arrival continues in the lobby, where Isoya Yoshida’s proportions and Western-style detailing remain part of the hotel’s contemporary life. Restored mouldings and joinery sit alongside brass, timber, textile and new lighting. Each layer remains visible, allowing the building’s age and renewal to be read together.
Behind the reception counter, a Nishijin textile folding screen provides the principal cultural gesture. Created using Fukurashi-ori, a three-dimensional weaving technique, its raised and recessed surfaces respond to light throughout the day. The composition captures the energy and changing density of water through textile, bringing Kyoto’s weaving tradition into the daily rituals of arrival and welcome.
Its position within reception gives craft an active role in the hotel. Guests experience the work while checking in, meeting companions or pausing in the lobby. Cultural knowledge is encountered through use and proximity, connecting artisanal practice with the social rhythms of an international hotel.
This dialogue between inherited architecture and contemporary craft gives the renewal its depth. The hotel’s history remains present through proportion, material and detail, while new commissions introduce a fresh cultural perspective. Design supports continuity across generations, allowing long-standing guests and first-time visitors to recognize the building as both established and current.
The journey to the guest rooms shifts the atmosphere from the lobby's social scale to a more personal one. Projected moonlight and reflections across the corridor floor guide movement with a gentle visual rhythm. The sequence introduces a sense of calm before guests enter their rooms.
Inside, the design draws water into the quieter rituals of rest. Green and ochre flooring changes in density around the bed, creating the impression of a surface in motion. The hotel’s signature green, brass lighting, restored mouldings, and soft upholstery give the rooms a composed, residential character that connects to the public spaces.
Calligraphic works extend across the headboards. Art becomes part of the guest’s daily experience, encountered while reading, resting and waking. Across all formats, artwork, materials, and light establish continuity with the wider hotel narrative.
For international visitors, Japanese culture is experienced through a sequence of encounters. Metal craft marks the entrance, Nishijin weaving shapes reception, projected light guides the corridors and calligraphy accompanies the guest room. These elements become part of arrival, movement and rest, giving cultural expression a direct relationship with hospitality.
RIHGA Royal Hotel Osaka enters its next era as both a cultural address and a place to stay. Its renewal connects design, art, craft, and hospitality with the life of the city, presenting Osaka through the movement of water, the continuity of architecture, and the rituals shared between the hotel and its guests.
